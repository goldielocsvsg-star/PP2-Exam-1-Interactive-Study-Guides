Nephron: The Functional Unit

▼
Key Numbers

~1.2 million nephrons per kidney
Each responsible for urine formation

🔄 Types of Nephrons

🟢
Cortical Nephrons

Located mainly in cortex
Short loops of Henle
Primarily filtration

🔴
Juxtamedullary Nephrons

Long loops deep in medulla
Essential for concentrated urine!

NCLEX Pearl

Juxtamedullary nephrons = concentrated urine
(Their long loops create the medullary gradient!)

🟠 Renal Corpuscle Components

  • Glomerulus — Capillary tuft for filtration
  • Bowman capsule — Cup that catches filtrate
  • Bowman space — Where filtrate collects
  • Mesangial cells — Support, phagocytosis, regulate blood flow

🧫 Glomerular Filtration Membrane Very Tested!

3 Layers (Outside → In)
  1. Fenestrated endothelium — Has holes (fenestrations)
  2. Basement membrane — Main barrier
  3. Podocytes with filtration slits — Final filter
⚠️ Why Proteins Are NOT Filtered:
  • Large molecular size
  • Negative charge repulsion (membrane is also negative)

⚡ Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA)

Component Function
Juxtaglomerular cells Release RENIN
Macula densa Senses NaCl concentration
Mesangial cells Support & regulation
JGA Regulates:

✓ Renal blood flow    ✓ GFR    ✓ Blood pressure    ✓ RAAS activation

🧪 Quick Check!

Which cells release renin?

Macula densa cells
Juxtaglomerular cells
Podocytes
Correct! JG cells release renin to activate RAAS.

Blood Supply & GFR NCLEX Favorite

▼

🩸 Blood Flow Order (Memorize This!)

Renal artery → Interlobar → Arcuate → Afferent arteriole → GLOMERULUS → Efferent arteriole → Peritubular / Vasa recta → Renal vein
💎 Vasa Recta

Preserves the medullary osmotic gradient — essential for urine concentration!

💡 "A before E"

Afferent = Approaches glomerulus (brings blood IN)
Efferent = Exits glomerulus (takes blood OUT)

📊 Key Numbers to Know

Kidneys receive: 20-25% of cardiac output

RBF: ~1000-1200 mL/min

GFR: ~120 mL/min

⚠️ NCLEX Alert!

Minimum urine output:
30 mL/hr

🎛️ Autoregulation of RBF

Purpose

Maintain constant GFR between BP 80-180 mmHg

Mechanism How It Works
Myogenic response Vessels constrict/dilate in response to pressure
Tubuloglomerular feedback Macula densa senses NaCl → adjusts afferent arteriole

🔄 Tubuloglomerular Feedback

↑ NaCl at macula densa
→ Afferent constriction
→ ↓ GFR

↓ NaCl at macula densa
→ Afferent dilation
→ ↑ GFR

🧪 Quick Check!

50 mL/hr
Correct! Less than 30 mL/hr indicates inadequate renal perfusion.

Neural & Hormonal Control

▼

🧠 Sympathetic Nervous System Effects

  • ↓ RBF (renal blood flow)
  • ↓ GFR
  • ↑ Na⁺ & water retention

🔴 RAAS (Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System)

⚡ RAAS Triggers

↓ BP   |   ↓ NaCl   |   Sympathetic stimulation

↓ BP/NaCl → JG cells release RENIN → Angiotensinogen → Angiotensin I → ACE → Angiotensin II → Aldosterone release
RAAS Effects:

✓ Vasoconstriction    ✓ Na⁺ retention    ✓ Water retention    ✓ ↑ BP

💙 Natriuretic Peptides (ANP/BNP)

OPPOSE RAAS!
→ Promote Na⁺ & water excretion
→ Lower blood pressure

💊 Hormones Affecting the Nephron

Hormone Action Result
ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone) ↑ water reabsorption in collecting duct Concentrated urine
Aldosterone ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption; ↑ K⁺ & H⁺ secretion ↑ Blood volume & BP

💡 ADH vs Aldosterone

ADH = "Anti-Diuretic" = Holds onto WATER 💧
Aldosterone = "Aldo-SALT-erone" = Holds onto SALT 🧂 (water follows)

🫘 Renal Hormones (Kidney Produces These!)

☀️ Vitamin D (Activated)

↑ Ca²⁺ & phosphate absorption
Deficiency in CKD!

🔴 Erythropoietin (EPO)

↑ RBC production
Deficiency → Anemia of CKD!

🧪 Quick Check!

Correct! ADH (antidiuretic hormone) makes collecting ducts permeable to water.

Ureters, Bladder, Urethra & Micturition

▼

📏 Ureters

  • ~30 cm long
  • Peristalsis moves urine
  • Pain referred to: Flank, umbilicus, genitals

🫧 Bladder

  • Detrusor muscle — Smooth muscle that contracts for urination
  • Transitional epithelium — Stretches!
  • Trigone — Smooth triangle between ureteral openings & urethra
📊 Know This Number!

Urge to void at 250-300 mL

🚪 Urethral Sphincters

Sphincter Type Control
Internal sphincter Smooth muscle INVOLUNTARY
External sphincter Skeletal muscle VOLUNTARY

💡 Sphincter Memory Trick

Internal = Involuntary (can't control it)
External = Exercise control (you choose!)

🔄 Micturition Reflex

Stretch receptors sense fullness → Signal to sacral spinal cord → Parasympathetic response → Bladder contracts + Internal sphincter relaxes

Voluntary control via external sphincter allows you to "hold it"!

Pediatric & Older Adult Considerations

▼
👶 Pediatric
  • Fewer concentrating nephrons
  • Dilute urine
  • ↑ Risk dehydration
  • GFR matures by 18-24 months
👴 Older Adults
  • ↓ Renal mass
  • ↓ Concentrating ability
  • ↑ Drug toxicity risk
  • ↑ CKD risk
⚠️ Clinical Alert

Older adults: Always assess renal function before giving nephrotoxic drugs!
Infants: Monitor closely for dehydration — they can't concentrate urine well!

Key Terms — Definitions

▼
Afferent arteriole
Blood vessel that carries blood INTO the glomerulus for filtration
Aldosterone
Hormone from the adrenal cortex that increases sodium and water reabsorption and increases potassium and hydrogen secretion
Antidiuretic hormone (ADH)
Hormone from posterior pituitary that increases water reabsorption in collecting ducts, concentrating urine
Autoregulation
Intrinsic mechanism that maintains stable RBF and GFR despite changes in systemic blood pressure
Bladder
Hollow muscular organ that stores urine and expels it during micturition
Bowman space
Space between the glomerulus and Bowman capsule where filtered fluid (primary urine) collects
Collecting duct
Final tubular segment that determines final urine concentration under the influence of ADH and aldosterone
Cortex
Outer region of the kidney containing glomeruli, proximal tubules, and parts of distal tubules
Countercurrent exchange system
Mechanism involving loops of Henle and vasa recta that creates and maintains the medullary osmotic gradient
Creatinine
Waste product of muscle metabolism that is freely filtered at the glomerulus; used to estimate GFR
Detrusor muscle
Smooth muscle layer of the bladder wall responsible for bladder contraction during urination
Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)
Tubular segment that fine-tunes electrolyte and acid-base balance; responds to aldosterone and ADH
Efferent arteriole
Blood vessel that carries blood AWAY from the glomerulus to the peritubular capillaries or vasa recta
Erythropoietin (EPO)
Hormone produced by the kidney that stimulates red blood cell production in bone marrow
Filtration slit
Narrow openings between podocyte foot processes that allow water and small solutes to pass while restricting proteins
Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)
Volume of plasma filtered by the kidneys per minute; indicator of kidney function (~120 mL/min normal)
Glomerulus
Tuft of capillaries within the renal corpuscle where filtration occurs
Hilum
Indented medial region of the kidney where vessels, nerves, lymphatics, and ureter enter and exit
Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)
Structure composed of macula densa, juxtaglomerular cells, and mesangial cells that regulates GFR and renin release
Juxtaglomerular cell
Modified smooth muscle cell in the afferent arteriole that secretes renin
Juxtamedullary nephron
Nephron with long loops of Henle extending deep into the medulla; essential for concentrated urine formation
Loop of Henle
Nephron segment that establishes the medullary osmotic gradient necessary for urine concentration
Macula densa
Specialized cells of the distal tubule that sense sodium chloride concentration and regulate GFR
Medulla
Inner region of the kidney containing pyramids, loops of Henle, and collecting ducts
Mesangial cell
Support cell within the glomerulus that regulates blood flow, provides structural support, and performs phagocytosis
Micturition
Process of bladder emptying (urination)
Natriuretic peptide
Hormone released from the heart that promotes sodium and water excretion and lowers blood pressure
Nephron
Functional unit of the kidney responsible for urine formation (~1.2 million per kidney)
Peritubular capillary
Capillary network surrounding renal tubules that facilitates reabsorption and secretion
Podocyte
Specialized epithelial cell of the glomerulus with foot processes forming filtration slits
Proximal tubule
Nephron segment that reabsorbs the majority (60-70%) of filtered water, sodium, and nutrients
Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)
Hormonal system that regulates blood pressure, sodium balance, and fluid volume
Renal corpuscle
Structure composed of the glomerulus and Bowman capsule where filtration occurs
Trigone
Smooth triangular region at the base of the bladder between ureteral openings and urethra
Tubular reabsorption
Movement of substances from tubular fluid back into the blood
Tubular secretion
Movement of substances from blood into the tubular fluid
Tubuloglomerular feedback
Autoregulatory mechanism by which macula densa cells adjust GFR based on sodium delivery
Ureter
Muscular tube (~30 cm) that transports urine from the kidney to the bladder via peristalsis
Urethra
Tube that carries urine from the bladder to the exterior of the body
Vasa recta
Capillary network that preserves the medullary osmotic gradient
Vitamin D
Hormone activated in the kidney that regulates calcium and phosphate balance
