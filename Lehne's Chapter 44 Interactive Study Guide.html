🎯 The Big Picture

Why do kidneys matter for diuretics? The kidneys maintain fluid + electrolyte balance by:

1️⃣ Cleansing ECF

Volume + composition control

2️⃣ Acid-Base Balance

Maintaining pH homeostasis

3️⃣ Waste Excretion

Drugs, toxins, metabolic waste

Diuretics mainly affect #1 (ECF volume/composition).

⚙️ The 3 Renal Processes

🔴 A. Filtration

  • Nonselective "sieve" at glomerulus
  • Filters: electrolytes, glucose, amino acids, drugs
  • Does NOT filter: cells, proteins, lipids
  • Rate: 125 mL/min ≈ 180 L/day

🟢 B. Reabsorption

  • >99% of filtered water/solutes reabsorbed
  • Solutes: active transport
  • Water: follows passively by osmosis
  • Most diuretics work here!

🟣 C. Secretion

  • Pumps move compounds plasma → lumen
  • Two pumps: organic acids + organic bases
  • Helps excrete wastes, drugs, toxins
180 L/day

Entire ECF (~12.5 L) is processed about 14 times per day (~every 100 min)!

Filtration can't control what stays in urine → Reabsorption + secretion decide final urine contents.

🧂 Where NaCl is Reabsorbed

💡 The earlier the site, the stronger the diuresis (more NaCl still available to block)!

📍

PCT

~65% NaCl

Reabsorbs all HCO₃⁻ + K⁺. Water follows → filtrate stays isotonic ~300 mOsm/L. Mannitol works here!

➰

Loop of Henle

~20% NaCl

Thick ascending limb. Not permeable to water → fluid becomes dilute. Furosemide works here!

🌀

Early DCT

~10% NaCl

Reabsorbs ~10% NaCl. Water follows. Thiazides (HCTZ) work here!

🎯

Distal Nephron

~1-5% Na⁺

Aldosterone ↑Na⁺ reabsorption + ↑K⁺ secretion. K⁺-sparing diuretics work here!

🧮 Quantitative Fact!

If kidneys filter ~180 L/day, then each 1% blockade of solute reabsorption → ~1.8 L/day extra urine! Small block = big fluid loss!

💊 Diuretic Drug Classes

🔵 Loop Diuretics — Furosemide (Lasix) ▼
📍 Site: Thick Ascending Limb ⚙️ MOA: Blocks NaCl reabsorption ⚡ STRONGEST DIURETIC

💉 Pharmacokinetics

  • PO: Onset ~60 min, lasts 6-8 h (non-emergent)
  • IV: Onset ~5 min, lasts ~2 h (emergent - pulmonary edema!)
  • Metabolized in liver, excreted by kidneys

✅ Clinical Uses

  • Pulmonary edema from CHF (rapid fluid mobilization)
  • Edema unresponsive to less potent agents
  • HTN resistant to other diuretics
  • Works even when GFR/renal blood flow is low!

⚠️ Major Adverse Effects

💧 Hyponatremia/Dehydration

Watch for: dry mouth, thirst, oliguria, rapid weight loss

📉 Hypotension

Symptoms: dizziness, light-headedness, fainting

⚡ Hypokalemia

DANGER: Fatal dysrhythmias if K⁺ < 3.5 mEq/L

👂 Ototoxicity

Rare, usually reversible with furosemide

⚡ Key Interactions

  • Digoxin: Hypokalemia ↑ risk of digoxin toxicity → ventricular dysrhythmias
  • Aminoglycosides: ↑ hearing risk
  • NSAIDs: Can blunt diuresis
  • Lithium: Toxicity risk
🟣 Thiazides — Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) ▼
📍 Site: Early DCT ⚙️ MOA: Blocks NaCl reabsorption (~10%) 💊 Often first-choice for HTN

💉 Pharmacokinetics

  • PO onset ~2 h, peak 4-6 h, lasts up to 12 h
  • Excreted largely unchanged in urine
  • Requires adequate kidney function!
  • ⚠️ Ineffective when GFR < 15-20 mL/min

✅ Clinical Uses

  • Essential hypertension (often first-choice!)
  • Edema in mild-moderate HF
  • Hepatic/renal edema

⚠️ Adverse Effects

Similar to loops EXCEPT: NO ototoxicity!

  • Dehydration, hypotension, hypokalemia
  • Hyperglycemia, hyperuricemia
  • Lipid changes
🟢 Potassium-Sparing Diuretics ▼

1️⃣ Aldosterone Antagonist — Spironolactone (Aldactone)

📍 Site: Distal Nephron ⚙️ Blocks aldosterone → ↓Na⁺ reabsorption, ↓K⁺ secretion
  • Diuresis: Mild (little Na⁺ left by distal nephron)
  • Onset: Delayed 24-48 h (must wait for existing transport proteins to turn over)
  • Uses: HTN + edema, often combined with loop/thiazide; severe HF (↓ mortality!)
⚡ HYPERKALEMIA (Big one!)

Stop if K⁺ > 5 mEq/L or dysrhythmia signs

🧬 Endocrine Effects

Gynecomastia, menstrual irregularities, impotence, hirsutism

2️⃣ Non-Aldosterone Antagonists — Triamterene / Amiloride

  • MOA: Directly inhibits Na⁺ entry/Na⁺-K⁺ exchange
  • Onset: Hours (faster than spironolactone)
  • Use: Mainly to prevent hypokalemia with loop/thiazide
  • Adverse: Hyperkalemia, GI upset, cramps, dizziness
⚠️ Watch for interactions with anything that raises K⁺: K⁺ supplements, salt substitutes, ACE inhibitors, ARBs!
🟠 Osmotic Diuretic — Mannitol (Osmitrol) ▼
⚙️ Filtered but NOT reabsorbed 📍 Holds water in tubule via osmosis 💉 IV ONLY

💉 Pharmacokinetics

  • Given IV only (not absorbed orally)
  • Onset: 30-60 min, lasts 6-8 h
  • Distributes to ECF, excreted intact
  • Minimal effect on K⁺ and other electrolytes

✅ Unique Uses

  • Prevent renal failure in low-flow states (keeps urine flowing)
  • Lower ICP (draws water out of brain - doesn't cross BBB)
  • Lower IOP (draws ocular fluid into blood)

⚠️ Major Dangers

💧 Worsened Edema

Leaves capillaries elsewhere and drags water with it

❤️ CHF / Pulmonary Edema

STOP immediately if pulmonary congestion/CHF signs appear!

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Implications

📊 Universal Monitoring

  • Weight daily (same scale, AM before eating)
  • BP (sitting + supine), pulse, respirations
  • Electrolytes: Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻
  • I&O, assess edema sites
  • Ascites: abdominal girth
  • Acute CHF: lung sounds

💊 Loop + Thiazide Priorities

  • Watch for dehydration signs
  • Monitor for hypotension (teach slow position changes)
  • Keep K⁺ ≥ 3.5 mEq/L (especially if on digoxin!)
  • Loop only: hearing changes

⏰ Administration Timing

  • Once daily: MORNING
  • Twice daily: 8am + 2pm (avoid nocturia)
  • IV furosemide: give slowly 1-2 min

🟢 K⁺-Sparing Priorities

  • Main risk: HYPERKALEMIA
  • Avoid K⁺ supplements, salt substitutes
  • Monitor ACEI/ARB/renin inhibitor use
  • Maintain K⁺ 3.5-5 mEq/L
  • Teach: restrict K⁺-rich foods if trending high

🧠 Final Memory Tips

🔑 Strength Rule

Early nephron drugs = stronger diuresis (more NaCl to block)
Distal nephron drugs = mild diuresis (little Na⁺ remains)

⚡ The Digoxin + Diuretic Danger

Loop/Thiazide → Hypokalemia → ↑ Digoxin toxicity → Ventricular dysrhythmias!
Always keep K⁺ ≥ 3.5 mEq/L in patients on digoxin!

🟢 K⁺-Sparing = K⁺ DANGER

Avoid: K⁺ supplements + salt substitutes + ACE inhibitors + ARBs + renin inhibitors
All of these ↑ K⁺ → Fatal hyperkalemia risk!
