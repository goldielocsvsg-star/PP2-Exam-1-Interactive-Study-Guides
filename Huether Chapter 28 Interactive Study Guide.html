1. Big Picture: What the Pulmonary System Does

▼
🌟 Primary Mission
Exchange gases between environmental air and blood with minimal energy!

The 3 Essential Steps of Gas Exchange

🌬️

Ventilation

Moving air IN and OUT of lungs

↔️

Diffusion

Moving gases between alveoli and blood

🩸

Perfusion

Moving blood through pulmonary capillaries to tissues

🏥 Clinical Anchor
Problems with ANY step → impaired oxygenation/CO₂ removal (hypoxemia and/or hypercapnia)

💡 Memory Tip: "VDP"

Ventilate → Diffuse → Perfuse
Think: "Very Deep Perfusion" - air goes Very Deep, then blood Perfuses!

🏗️

2. Structures of the Pulmonary System

▼

🧠 Remember: Know the Map!

Understanding structure = understanding function!

🫁 Major Components

  • Lungs — Right: 3 lobes | Left: 2 lobes (heart takes space!)
  • Upper airway — Nasopharynx, oropharynx, related structures
  • Lower airway — Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles
  • Gas-exchange units — Respiratory bronchioles → Alveolar ducts → Alveoli
  • Pulmonary blood vessels + lymphatics
  • Diaphragm + chest wall (thoracic cage)
  • Pleura & pleural space
  • Mediastinum — Space between lungs: heart, great vessels, esophagus

🎯 Quick Trick: "3-2 Rule"

Right lung = 3 lobes | Left lung = 2 lobes
Left has Less because the heart Lives there!

3. Pulmonary Defenses (Why Lungs Stay "Clean")

▼
🎯 Goal
Warm, humidify, trap, neutralize, and remove invaders before they reach alveoli!

🔑 Key Defense Mechanisms (HIGH YIELD!)

Defense Function
🔸 Upper respiratory mucosa / nasal hairs / turbinates Trap particles + humidify/heat air
🔸 Mucous blanket Protective gel that traps pathogens/irritants
🔸 Cilia Move mucus upward toward pharynx ("Mucociliary Escalator")
🔸 Cough & sneeze reflexes Triggered by irritant receptors
🔸 Surfactant Helps immune defense in alveoli + dampens inflammation
🔸 Macrophages (alveolar + interstitial) Phagocytosis + cytokines + antigen presentation
🔸 Innate immune proteins Lysozyme, lactoferrin, defensins, SP-A/D, IgA

🎢 The Mucociliary Escalator

Think of it like an escalator at the mall — cilia are the moving steps constantly pushing mucus (with trapped junk) UP and OUT! 🛗

🏥 Clinical Anchor
Impaired cilia/mucus clearance → infections, chronic bronchitis, CF, etc.
🌬️

4. Conducting Airways (Air Moves, No Gas Exchange)

▼

👃 Upper Airway

  • Nasopharynx & oropharynx: Lined with ciliated mucosa → warms/humidifies/filters air
  • Mouth breathing: Used if nose obstructed or high demand (exercise) but LESS filtering/humidifying

🎤 Larynx (Gateway + Aspiration Prevention)

  • False vocal cords (supraglottis)
  • True vocal cords with glottis between
  • Vestibule above false cords
  • Cartilage support prevents collapse
💎 Swallow Protection
Coordinated closure of cords + vestibule + epiglottis prevents aspiration!

🔧 Laryngeal Muscles

Internal laryngeal muscles:
Tension/length of cords; close during swallowing
External muscles:
Move larynx overall

🌳 Trachea → Bronchi

  • Trachea: C-shaped cartilage rings keep airway open
  • Splits at carina → right & left main bronchi
  • Bronchi enter lungs at hila with blood & lymph vessels

🧱 Bronchial Wall — 3 Layers

Layer Key Components
1️⃣ Epithelial (mucosal) lining Goblet cells, ciliated columnar cells, club cells, basal cells, neuroendocrine cells
2️⃣ Submucosal layer (lamina propria) Smooth muscle, submucosal glands, autonomic innervation
3️⃣ Outer cartilaginous layer Structural support

Goblet Cells

🍷

Tap to flip!

Secrete mucins (MUC5B, MUC5AC) → form mucus gel barrier that traps pathogens!

Ciliated Columnar Cells

🌊

Tap to flip!

Beat mucus upward toward pharynx — the "mucociliary escalator" for clearance!

Club Cells

⭐

Tap to flip!

Secrete immune molecules + protective surfactant-type proteins!

Basal Cells

🔄

Tap to flip!

Regenerate new ciliated cells — the stem cells of the airway!

⚡ Lamina Propria - HIGH YIELD
  • Smooth muscle: Increases as airways get smaller; GREATEST at terminal bronchiole
  • Submucosal glands: Secrete mucins (MUC5B) — important in CF pathology
  • Parasympathetic (vagus): Bronchoconstriction + ↑ secretions
  • Sympathetic: Bronchodilation
🏥 Clinical Anchor
Asthma meds often block parasympathetic and/or stimulate sympathetic (β-agonists)

5-6. Gas-Exchange Airways (Where O₂/CO₂ Actually Swap)

▼

🫧 The Acinus (Gas Exchange Region)

📐 Conducting Airways End Here →

Respiratory Bronchioles → Alveolar Ducts → Alveoli
= The ACINUS (collectively)

🔴 Alveoli Essentials

📊 Numbers to Know
~25 million at birth → ~300 million in adulthood
Cell Type Function
Type I Alveolar Cells Thin structure, prevent fluid leakage into alveoli
Type II Alveolar Cells Make surfactant + repair epithelium + help fluid/ion transport
Alveolar Macrophages First-line defense in alveolar space
Interstitial Macrophages Prevent spread into interstitium/capillaries

💡 Type I vs Type II

Type 1 = 1 thin layer (gas exchange)
Type 2 = 2 jobs (surfactant + repair)

🧴 Surfactant Proteins

SP-B & SP-C:
Lower surface tension (keep alveoli open!)
SP-A & SP-D:
Collectins → innate immune defense
💎 Pores of Kohn
Collateral ventilation between alveoli — helps even air distribution!

🔬 Alveolar-Capillary Membrane

  • Ultra-thin interface where diffusion occurs
  • Thickening or fluid in interstitium → impaired diffusion → hypoxemia

🧪 Quick Check!

Which cell type produces surfactant?

Type I Alveolar Cells
Type II Alveolar Cells
Goblet Cells
Correct! Type II cells make surfactant AND repair the epithelium.

7-8. Pulmonary vs Bronchial Circulation

▼
⚠️ Don't Mix These Up!
Two different circulation systems with different purposes!
Feature 🔵 Pulmonary Circulation 🔴 Bronchial Circulation
Purpose GAS EXCHANGE Support/nutrients (NO gas exchange)
Path Right heart → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left heart Supplies conducting airways, large vessels, lymph nodes, pleura
Pressure LOW pressure/LOW resistance Systemic pressure
Functions Gas exchange, nutrient delivery, reservoir for LV, filter (clots/air/debris) Nutritional support only

🧅 Lymphatics

  • Deep & superficial lymphatics remove fluid + macrophages
  • Drain through mediastinal nodes
  • Key role: Prevent pulmonary edema + support immunity

⚡ Control of Pulmonary Blood Flow

🚨 HUGE TEST POINT: Hypoxic Pulmonary Vasoconstriction
  • Low PAO₂ → pulmonary arteriolar CONSTRICTION
  • Purpose: Shunt blood AWAY from poorly ventilated areas → improve V/Q matching
  • If widespread hypoxia → global constriction → pulmonary hypertension
  • Chronic hypoxia → structural remodeling → permanent pulmonary HTN → cor pulmonale (right HF)

🧠 Remember: "Hypoxia = Constriction in Lungs"

This is the OPPOSITE of systemic circulation!
Lungs: Low O₂ = vasoconstrict (redirect blood)
Systemic: Low O₂ = vasodilate (bring more blood)

💊 Other Vasoconstrictors/Modulators

  • Acidemia also constricts pulmonary arteries
  • Histamine, prostaglandins, serotonin, nitric oxide, bradykinin influence tone

🦴 Chest Wall

  • Provides muscles for ventilation (intercostals, accessory, abdominal)

10. Ventilation (Mechanics + Key Numbers)

▼
⚠️ Common Trap!
Ventilation = Air movement
Respiration = Cellular O₂/CO₂ exchange during metabolism
They're NOT the same!
📐 Minute Ventilation Formula

V̇ = RR × VT

Tidal Volume (VT): Air per breath (~500 mL in quiet breathing)

🚫 Dead Space Ventilation (VD)

Air that doesn't participate in gas exchange:

Anatomic dead space:
Conducting airways
Alveolar dead space:
Alveoli not perfused (ventilation without perfusion)
🩺 CO₂ Removal + Acid-Base
  • Lungs remove CO₂ (carbonic acid equivalent) to maintain PaCO₂ ~ 40 mm Hg
  • Hypoventilation → CO₂ retention → ↑ PaCO₂ (respiratory acidosis)
  • You can't judge ventilation adequacy by looking → need ABG or capnography!
🧠

11-13. Neurochemical Control of Breathing

▼

🧠 Respiratory Center (Medulla + Pons)

Adjusts RR + VT based on chemoreceptors and lung receptor feedback.

Component Function
DRG (Dorsal Respiratory Group) Initiates inspiration; rhythm maintenance; responds to peripheral chemo + lung inputs
VRG (Ventral Respiratory Group) Modulates inspiration & expiration; contains pre-Bötzinger complex (rhythm generator)
Pneumotaxic center Regulates inspiratory volume + switches inspiration → expiration
Apneustic center Moderates/inhibits inspiration intensity
💊 Clinical Pearl
Opioids suppress VRG activity → ↓ RR (classic opioid respiratory depression)

📡 Lung Receptors (3 Types = TEST GOLD!)

Irritant Receptors (C-fibers)

😤

Tap for details!

Location: Conducting airway epithelium
Trigger: Dust, fumes, gases, particulates
Effects: Cough, bronchoconstriction, ↑ ventilatory rate

Stretch Receptors

🎈

Tap for details!

Location: Airway smooth muscle + chest wall
Sense: Lung inflation
Effect: ↓ rate/volume when stimulated (Hering-Breuer reflex)

J-Receptors

💗

Tap for details!

Location: Near alveolar capillaries/septa
Trigger: ↑ pulmonary capillary pressure
Effects: Rapid shallow breathing, hypotension, bradycardia

🔬 Chemoreceptors

Type Central Peripheral
Location Near respiratory center; sense CSF pH Carotid bodies & aortic bodies
Primary Sensitivity CO₂ (indirectly via H⁺ in CSF) LOW PaO₂
Key Point ↑ CO₂ → crosses BBB → forms H⁺ → ↓ pH → ↑ ventilation Significant stimulation when PaO₂ ~ 60 mm Hg or lower
🏥 COPD Alert
In chronic hypercapnia (e.g., COPD): central receptors can reset and become less responsive; kidneys retain HCO₃⁻ which buffers CSF → peripheral chemoreceptors become dominant ventilatory drive!

14. Mechanics of Breathing (3 Things Determine Work)

▼

💪 A. Muscles

Phase Primary Muscles Accessory Muscles
Inspiration Diaphragm + external intercostals Sternocleidomastoid + scalenes (high demand/disease)
Expiration Normally PASSIVE! Accessory: Abdominal + internal intercostals (coughing, obstruction, high V̇)

🫧 B. Alveolar Surface Tension + Surfactant

  • Surface tension tends to collapse alveoli
  • Law of Laplace: Smaller radius (end-expiration) needs MORE pressure to inflate → collapse risk
  • Surfactant (Type II cells) reduces surface tension → prevents collapse, decreases work of breathing
👶 Clinical Application
Lack of surfactant → collapse + severe gas exchange issues
Classic in preterm infants!

🔄 C. Elastic Recoil + Compliance

Elastic recoil: Lung returns to resting state → allows passive expiration

Compliance: "Stretchiness" (volume change per pressure change)
Compliance Level What It Means Examples
⬆️ HIGH compliance Easy to inflate, poor recoil Emphysema
⬇️ LOW compliance Stiff lungs ARDS, pneumonia, pulmonary edema, fibrosis

🚧 D. Airway Resistance

  • Depends on airway radius/length, gas properties
  • Biggest resistance normally: Nose (then oropharynx/larynx)
  • Resistance increases with: mucosal edema, mucus plugging, tumors, foreign bodies
  • Diffuse ↑ resistance → ↑ work → hypercapnia risk
Parasympathetic:
→ Bronchoconstriction 🔒
Sympathetic:
→ Bronchodilation 🔓
📈 E. Work of Breathing RISES When:
  • ↓ lung compliance (pulmonary edema)
  • ↓ chest wall compliance (obesity, spinal deformity)
  • Airway obstruction (asthma/COPD)

→ Can cause ↑ O₂ demand + fatigue → hypoventilation → hypercapnia

15-20. Gas Transport & The O₂-Hemoglobin Curve

▼

🔵 Oxygen Transport (4 Steps)

1️⃣

Ventilation

2️⃣

O₂ Diffusion

Alveoli → pulmonary capillary blood

3️⃣

Perfusion

Systemic capillaries

4️⃣

O₂ Diffusion

Capillary → cells

Key concept: Any failure in steps → cellular hypoxia!

🔴 CO₂ Transport (Reverse!)

Cells → systemic blood → pulmonary blood → alveoli → exhaled

📊 PaO₂ vs SaO₂ (DON'T CONFUSE!)

Measurement What It Is How Measured
PaO₂ Dissolved O₂ pressure in plasma ABG
SaO₂ % hemoglobin saturated Pulse ox
~97% O₂ binds hemoglobin, ~3% dissolved in plasma
Low Hgb → low O₂ content even if PaO₂ looks okay!

📈 Oxyhemoglobin Dissociation Curve

The curve shows relation between PaO₂ and SaO₂

Shift Direction ➡️ RIGHT SHIFT ⬅️ LEFT SHIFT
Effect Easier O₂ release (↓ affinity) Tighter binding (↑ affinity)
Causes • Acidosis (↓ pH)
• Hypercapnia (↑ PaCO₂)
• ↑ Temperature
• ↑ 2,3-DPG 		• Alkalosis
• Hypocapnia
• ↓ Temperature
• ↓ 2,3-DPG
Clinical Good for tissues! Holds O₂ too tightly (less delivered)

💡 Bohr Effect

CO₂/H⁺ cause RIGHT shift = enhances tissue unloading!
"Working tissues are acidic and warm — they need that O₂!"

🔴 CO₂ Transport (3 Forms) + Haldane Effect

CO₂ is ~20× more soluble than O₂ → diffuses easily!

CO₂ Carried As:
1️⃣ Dissolved in plasma
2️⃣ Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) — MAJOR form!
3️⃣ Carbamino compounds (bound to proteins/hemoglobin)

💡 Haldane Effect

Deoxygenated hemoglobin carries MORE CO₂ (tissues load CO₂ better)
Oxygenation in lungs promotes CO₂ unloading!

💎 Pearl
Diffusion defects often cause hypoxemia BEFORE hypercapnia because CO₂ diffuses so well!

⚖️ V̇/Q̇ Matching (Major Cause of Hypoxemia!)

Location Ventilation Perfusion
Bases More (more compliant) Even MORE (gravity)
Apices More than perfusion Less (against gravity)
Normal V̇/Q̇ ≈ 0.8
V/Q mismatch = #1 cause of hypoxemia in many lung diseases!

21. Aging & Pulmonary System

▼
🎯 Older Adult Must-Know!
  • Chest wall compliance ↓ (stiffer ribs/joints) → ↑ work of breathing
  • Elastic recoil ↓ → lung compliance ↑, VC ↓, RV ↑, TLC ~ unchanged
  • Peripheral airway support ↓ + epithelial regeneration ↓ + distal airway fibrosis ↑
  • Gas exchange surface/perfusion ↓ (capillary network diminishes), alveoli dilate/fibrose
  • PaO₂ declines with age; ventilatory response to hypoxia/hypercapnia ↓
  • Lung immunity declines → ↑ infection risk
📐 Estimation Tip

Max PaO₂ ≈ 100 − (age × 0.3) at sea level

💪 Good News!

Fitness protects function!
Sedentary lifestyle → more decline. Stay active!

Key Terms (High-Yield Definitions)

▼
Acinus
Functional gas-exchange unit (respiratory bronchioles + alveolar ducts + alveoli)
Alveolar duct
Passageway leading from respiratory bronchioles to alveolar sacs
Alveolar ventilation (VA)
Fresh air reaching alveoli that participates in gas exchange
Alveolar-capillary membrane
Thin diffusion barrier between alveoli and capillary blood
Alveolus (alveoli)
Tiny air sac where gas exchange occurs
Bohr effect
CO₂/H⁺ shifts O₂ dissociation curve (usually right) to enhance tissue unloading
Bronchus (bronchi)
Large conducting airway branching from trachea into lungs
Carina
Tracheal bifurcation point into main bronchi; sensitive → cough reflex
Central chemoreceptor
Brainstem receptor sensing CSF pH changes driven by CO₂
Collectins
Surfactant proteins (SP-A, SP-D) that bind pathogens and support innate immunity
Compliance
Ease of lung/chest wall expansion (ΔV/ΔP); inverse of stiffness
Dead-space ventilation (VD)
Ventilated air not participating in gas exchange (anatomic + alveolar)
Elastic recoil
Tendency of lungs/chest wall to return to resting volume after inspiration
Fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO₂)
% O₂ in inhaled air (room air ≈ 0.21)
Glottis
Opening between true vocal cords
Goblet cell
Airway epithelial cell that secretes mucins to form mucus
Haldane effect
Oxygenation of blood in lungs promotes CO₂ release; deoxygenation in tissues promotes CO₂ uptake
Hilum (hila)
"Root" of lung where bronchi, vessels, lymphatics enter/exit
Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction
Low PAO₂ triggers pulmonary arteriolar constriction to redirect blood
Irritant receptor
Airway receptor sensing noxious particles/gases → cough, bronchoconstriction
J-receptor
Receptor near pulmonary capillaries responding to ↑ capillary pressure → rapid shallow breathing
Lamina propria
Submucosal bronchial layer containing smooth muscle, glands, ANS fibers
Larynx
Airway structure between pharynx and trachea; protects airway, enables vocalization
Mediastinum
Thoracic space between lungs containing heart/great vessels/esophagus
Minute volume (V̇)
RR × VT (L/min)
Oxygen saturation (SaO₂)
% hemoglobin binding sites occupied by O₂
Oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve
S-shaped relation between PaO₂ and SaO₂; shifts with pH/CO₂/temp/2,3-DPG
Partial pressure (P)
Pressure exerted by an individual gas in a mixture
Peripheral chemoreceptor
Carotid/aortic body receptors mainly sensing low PaO₂
Pleura (pleurae)
Serous membranes (visceral + parietal) around lungs/thorax
Pleural space
Thin fluid-filled space between pleurae; normally negative pressure
Respiratory bronchiole
First airway segment participating in gas exchange
Respiratory center
Medulla/pons network controlling RR and VT
Stretch receptor
Airway/chest wall receptors sensing lung inflation and ventilatory mechanics
Surface tension
Force at air-liquid interface tending to collapse alveoli
Surfactant
Lipoprotein from Type II cells lowering alveolar surface tension; prevents collapse; supports immunity
Tidal volume (VT)
Volume inhaled/exhaled per normal breath
Trachea
Cartilage-supported airway from larynx to bronchi
Ventilation
Movement of air into/out of lungs
Ventilation-perfusion ratio (V̇/Q̇)
Relationship of airflow to blood flow; mismatch → hypoxemia
