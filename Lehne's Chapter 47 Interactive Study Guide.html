🔹 BIG PICTURE: What Does RAAS Regulate?
🩸 Blood Pressure
💧 Blood Volume

RAAS activates during: Hypotension, Dehydration, Hemorrhage, Low sodium states

2️⃣ Sodium + Water retention

Key effectors: Angiotensin II & Aldosterone

RAAS Step-by-Step - KNOW THIS FLOW!
The complete cascade
The RAAS Cascade
1️⃣ ↓ BP / ↓ Volume / ↓ Na⁺ / ↓ Renal Perfusion
→
2️⃣ Kidney releases RENIN
→
3️⃣ Renin converts Angiotensinogen → Angiotensin I (inactive)
→
4️⃣ ACE converts Ang I → Angiotensin II (ACTIVE!)
→
5️⃣ Ang II → Vasoconstriction + Aldosterone release
→
6️⃣ Aldosterone → Na⁺ & H₂O retention → ↑ BP!
Where the Drugs Block!
ACE Inhibitors = Block step 4 (Ang I → Ang II)
ARBs = Block Ang II at receptors (step 5)
Aliskiren = Block step 3 (FIRST step - Renin)
Aldosterone blockers = Block step 6
🔑
Renin - The Rate-Limiting Step
Released from juxtaglomerular cells
▼
🔔 Renin Release TRIGGERED by:
  • ↓ Blood pressure
  • ↓ Blood volume
  • ↓ Sodium
  • ↓ Renal perfusion
  • β₁-sympathetic stimulation
🛑 Renin Release SUPPRESSED by:
  • ↑ Blood pressure
  • ↑ Blood volume
  • ↑ Sodium
= Negative Feedback Loop!
🧪
ACE (Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme)
Also called Kinase II!
▼
⚙️ What ACE Does
  • Converts Ang I → Ang II
  • Found in ALL vessels (lungs = highest!)
  • Also breaks down BRADYKININ
ACE Inhibitor Double Action!
ACE Inhibitors:
↓ Angiotensin II (less vasoconstriction)
↑ Bradykinin (causes COUGH!)
⏰
How RAAS Raises BP - EXAM CLASSIC!
Two speeds!
▼
Speed Mechanism Time Frame
FAST ⚡ Vasoconstriction Minutes to Hours
SLOW 🐢 Na⁺ & Water Retention Days to Weeks
⚡
Angiotensin II - 3 Major Actions ⭐⭐⭐
The MOST important molecule!
1️⃣ VASOCONSTRICTION
  • Strong arteriolar constriction
  • ↑ Systemic vascular resistance → ↑ BP
  • Also increases: Norepinephrine, Epinephrine, Sympathetic outflow
2️⃣ ALDOSTERONE RELEASE
  • Stimulates adrenal cortex
  • ↑ Na⁺ & water retention
  • ↑ K⁺ excretion
  • Raises BP even at LOW Ang II levels!
3️⃣ CARDIAC & VASCULAR REMODELING (PATHOLOGIC!)
  • Cardiac hypertrophy
  • Fibrosis
  • Vessel wall thickening
  • Worsens: HTN, HF, MI, Atherosclerosis!
Ang II = THE VILLAIN!
Everything it does chronically is BAD:
- Raises BP (bad for heart)
- Causes remodeling (bad for vessels)
- That's why we BLOCK it with drugs!
🧂
Aldosterone - Key Effects
Good vs. Bad!
▼
✅ Normal Effects
  • Acts on distal renal tubules
  • ↑ Na⁺ & water retention
  • ↑ K⁺ & H⁺ excretion
  • ↑ Blood volume → ↑ BP
VS
❌ Harmful (High Levels)
  • Cardiac remodeling & fibrosis
  • ↑ Dysrhythmia risk
  • ↓ Arterial compliance
  • ↑ Sympathetic activity
📌 Important!
Harmful effects seen mainly in HEART FAILURE!
💊
ACE Inhibitors (-prils)
Captopril, Enalapril, Lisinopril, Ramipril
⚙️ Mechanism
↓ Angiotensin II (less vasoconstriction)
↑ Bradykinin (vasodilation... but also COUGH!)
👍 Effects
  • Vasodilation
  • ↓ Blood volume
  • ↓ Remodeling (protects heart!)
  • ↑ K⁺ (hyperkalemia risk!)
⭐ Major Uses - HIGH YIELD!
  • Hypertension
  • Heart Failure
  • Post-MI
  • Diabetic & Nondiabetic Nephropathy
  • CV Risk Reduction (ramipril, perindopril)
⚠️
ACE Inhibitor Adverse Effects
Common vs. Serious!
▼
😷 Common Adverse Effects
  • DRY COUGH (due to ↑ bradykinin)
  • HYPERKALEMIA
  • FIRST-DOSE HYPOTENSION
🚨 Serious Adverse Effects!
  • ANGIOEDEMA - STOP FOREVER if occurs!
  • RENAL FAILURE - In renal artery stenosis!
  • FETAL INJURY - Contraindicated in pregnancy!
Cough? Switch to ARB!
ACE inhibitor cough is from bradykinin.
ARBs don't affect bradykinin = NO COUGH!
💊
ARBs (-sartans)
Losartan, Valsartan, Candesartan
⚙️ Mechanism
Block Angiotensin II receptors
Do NOT increase bradykinin!

ARB = "A Really Better" option for cough!
Patient on ACE inhibitor with cough? → Switch to ARB!

Aliskiren - Direct Renin Inhibitor (DRI)
Blocks the FIRST step of RAAS!
Where Aliskiren Works
❌ RENIN
(BLOCKED HERE!)
→
Angiotensinogen → Ang I
→
Entire RAAS Suppressed!
✅ Clinical Use
HYPERTENSION ONLY!

No proven benefit for:
• Heart failure
• Diabetic nephropathy
• Mortality reduction

Older drugs preferred due to better outcome data
👍 Advantages vs. ACE Inhibitors
  • Much LESS cough!
  • Lower angioedema risk
⚠️ Adverse Effects
• DIARRHEA (dose-dependent, worse at 300 mg)
• HYPERKALEMIA (esp. with ACEI, ARBs, K⁺ supplements)
• ANGIOEDEMA (rare but serious)
• FETAL INJURY & DEATH
💊 Pharmacokinetics
  • PO only
  • Very LOW bioavailability
  • High-fat meals ↓ absorption!
  • Take consistently with respect to meals
  • Half-life ≈ 24 hours
💊
Aldosterone Antagonists
Eplerenone vs. Spironolactone
⚙️ Mechanism
Block aldosterone receptors →
• ↑ Na⁺ & water excretion
• ↑ K⁺ retention (HYPERKALEMIA risk!)
• ↓ Blood volume & BP
• ↓ Cardiac remodeling & fibrosis
Eplerenone (PREFERRED)
  • More selective
  • HTN (add-on therapy)
  • HF (↓ mortality!)
  • Fewer sex hormone side effects
VS
Spironolactone
  • Less selective
  • MORE side effects:
  • • Gynecomastia
  • • Menstrual irregularities
  • • Impotence
  • • Hirsutism
⚠️ BOTH Cause HYPERKALEMIA!
Contraindications for Eplerenone:
• K⁺ > 5.5 mEq/L
• Renal impairment
• Type 2 diabetes with microalbuminuria
Spironolactone = Sex Hormone Effects!
Remember: SPIRO = SPERM Issues!
(gynecomastia, impotence, etc.)

Nursing Implications - EXAM GOLD!
For ALL RAAS inhibitors

  • BLOOD PRESSURE
  • SERUM POTASSIUM
  • RENAL FUNCTION
📚 Patient Education
  • Watch for dizziness (hypotension)
  • Signs of angioedema (face/tongue swelling)

  • Report persistent cough (ACE inhibitors)

ALL RAAS BLOCKERS = NO PREGNANCY!

Contraindicated in 2nd & 3rd trimesters (potential risk in 1st)
Can cause: Fetal hypotension, Renal failure, Skull hypoplasia, DEATH
STOP IMMEDIATELY if pregnancy occurs!

• Tongue
• Lips
• Face
• Throat (airway emergency!)

ACTION: STOP DRUG IMMEDIATELY!
NEVER restart same drug class!
🫘
Renal Artery Stenosis - RED FLAG!
When NOT to use RAAS blockers
▼
⚠️ Can Cause SUDDEN RENAL FAILURE!
ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and Aliskiren are CONTRAINDICATED in:

• Bilateral renal artery stenosis
• Stenosis to a single remaining kidney

Use with EXTREME caution or AVOID!
Why Does This Happen?
In renal artery stenosis, kidneys DEPEND on Ang II to constrict efferent arteriole and maintain GFR.
Block Ang II → GFR crashes → Renal failure!
