💊 Diuretics

🧠 Memory Trick

"LOOP makes you LOSE potassium!"

Loop diuretics like Lasix cause hypoKalemia - think of the potassium going down the drain with all that urine!

Furosemide (Lasix)
Loop Diuretic
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Inhibits Na/Cl reabsorption in Loop of Henle → ↑ urine output

📋 Use:

Fluid overload, HTN, edema, CHF, pulmonary edema

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • HypoKalemia (CHECK K+ FIRST!)
  • Dehydration, hypotension
  • Ototoxicity (high doses)
🩺 Nursing:
  • Give AM (prevent nocturia)
  • Monitor I&O, daily weights
  • Eat bananas! 🍌 (K+ rich foods)
Mannitol
Osmotic Diuretic
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Pulls fluid from tissues → bloodstream → kidneys

📋 Use:

↑ ICP, acute glaucoma, AKI prevention

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Pulmonary edema (watch CHF!)
  • Dehydration
  • Electrolyte imbalance
🩺 Nursing:
  • Use IV filter (crystallizes!)
  • Monitor lung sounds
  • Check serum osmolality
  • Assess for dehydration

🦴 Phosphate Binders

🧠 Memory Trick

"Phosphate Binders = Bind with FOOD!"

ALL phosphate binders MUST be given WITH MEALS to bind dietary phosphate in the GI tract!

Calcium Acetate
Phosphate Binder
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Binds dietary phosphate in GI → ↓ absorption

📋 Use:

Hyperphosphatemia in CKD

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • HyperCALCemia (monitor Ca!)
  • Constipation
  • Arrhythmias, confusion
🩺 Nursing:
  • GIVE WITH MEALS!
  • Monitor Ca & PO4 labs
  • Avoid vitamin D overdose
  • Teach low-phosphate diet
Sevelamer
Non-Calcium Binder
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Polymer binds phosphate in intestines

📋 Use:

Hyperphosphatemia in dialysis (no calcium increase!)

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Constipation/diarrhea
  • Nausea, abdominal pain
🩺 Nursing:
  • GIVE WITH MEALS!
  • Don't crush tablets
  • Monitor lipids (↓ LDL bonus!)
  • Best for patients who can't tolerate Ca
Lanthanum Carbonate
Non-Calcium Binder
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Binds phosphate without increasing calcium

📋 Use:

CKD hyperphosphatemia (calcium intolerant patients)

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Nausea, vomiting
  • Diarrhea
  • Abdominal pain
🩺 Nursing:
  • CHEW tablets fully!
  • Give with meals
  • Monitor phosphate levels

🌟 Vitamins & Supplements

Ferrous Sulfate
Iron Supplement
💡 Click to see details!
📋 Use:

Iron-deficiency anemia in CKD

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Constipation
  • Dark/black stools (normal!)
  • GI upset, nausea
🩺 Nursing:
  • Give with Vitamin C (↑ absorption)
  • NOT with milk or antacids!
  • Use straw for liquid (teeth stains)
  • Teach about black stools
✅ Expected:

Increased Hgb/Hct

Calcitriol
Active Vitamin D
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

↑ Ca absorption; suppresses PTH

📋 Use:

CKD can't activate Vitamin D → secondary hyperparathyroidism

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • HyperCALCemia
  • Hyperphosphatemia
  • Kidney stones
🩺 Nursing:
  • Monitor Ca & phosphate closely
  • Watch for kidney stones
  • Check PTH levels
Folic Acid
Vitamin B9
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

DNA synthesis, RBC production

📋 Use:

Anemia, dialysis losses, pregnancy

⚠️ Side Effects:

Rare - nausea, rash

🩺 Nursing:
  • Safe in pregnancy
  • Check B12 first!
  • Monitor RBC production
Cyanocobalamin
Vitamin B12
💡 Click to see details!
📋 Use:

RBC production, nerve function, pernicious anemia

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • HypoKalemia (early therapy)
  • Injection site pain
🩺 Nursing:
  • Often IM injections LIFELONG
  • Monitor potassium
  • Check neurologic symptoms
✅ Expected:

Improved Hgb/Hct, better neuro function

🔴 ESAs & PTH Modulators

🧠 Critical Memory Trick

"Epoetin = Check IRON first & watch BP!"

Target Hgb 10-11 g/dL (NOT normal!) - Overcorrection = ↑ stroke/MI risk! 🔥

Epoetin Alfa
ESA - RBC Stimulator
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Stimulates bone marrow → produce RBCs

📋 Use:

Anemia in CKD and dialysis

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • HYPERTENSION 📈
  • Thromboembolism
  • Headache, seizures
🩺 Nursing:
  • CHECK IRON STORES FIRST!
  • Monitor BP closely
  • Target Hgb 10-11 g/dL
  • 🔥 OVERCORRECTION = stroke/MI!
Cinacalcet
Calcimimetic
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

↓ PTH by increasing Ca receptor sensitivity

📋 Use:

Secondary hyperparathyroidism in CKD

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • HypoCALCemia (watch closely!)
  • Nausea, vomiting
  • SEIZURES
🩺 Nursing:
  • Monitor Ca FREQUENTLY
  • Teach s/s hypocalcemia:
  • - Tetany, tingling
  • - Seizures, muscle spasms
Vancomycin
Glycopeptide Antibiotic
💡 Click to see details!
📋 Use:

Serious gram+ infections (MRSA)

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • NEPHROTOXICITY ⚠️
  • Ototoxicity
  • Red Man Syndrome
🩺 Nursing:
  • TROUGH BEFORE dose!
  • Monitor peak & trough
  • Infuse SLOWLY
  • Monitor BUN/Cr closely

🚨 Hyperkalemia Emergency Protocol

⚡ MEMORIZE THIS ORDER! ⚡

STEP 1: Protect the Heart 💓

Calcium Gluconate

Stabilizes cardiac membrane - NO effect on K+ level!

Watch: ECG, arrhythmias, phlebitis

STEP 2: Shift K+ Into Cells 🔄

Regular Insulin + Dextrose (D50W)

Insulin drives K into cells; Dextrose prevents hypoglycemia

Watch: Glucose levels, K+ levels

STEP 3: Adjunct Therapy 🌬️

Albuterol (inhaled) OR Sodium Bicarb

Albuterol: β2-agonist shifts K into cells

Bicarb: Corrects acidosis, shifts K

Watch: HR (albuterol), pH/electrolytes (bicarb)

STEP 4: Remove K+ from Body 🚽

Kayexalate OR Dialysis

Kayexalate: Exchanges Na for K in GI tract

Watch: Bowel obstruction, intestinal necrosis

Calcium Gluconate
Cardiac Protector
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Stabilizes cardiac membrane during hyperkalemia

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Arrhythmias
  • Phlebitis
  • Constipation
🩺 Nursing:
  • ECG MONITORING!
  • IV site assessment
  • First-line cardiac protection
✅ Expected:

Normal sinus rhythm on ECG

Insulin + D50W
K+ Shifter
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Insulin moves K into cells; D50 prevents hypoglycemia

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Hypoglycemia
  • Hypokalemia (from shift)
  • Hyperglycemia (from dextrose)
🩺 Nursing:
  • ALWAYS give together!
  • Monitor glucose closely
  • Recheck K+ levels
Kayexalate
K+ Remover
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Exchanges Na for K in GI → excreted in stool

📋 Use:

Chronic hyperkalemia management

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Diarrhea
  • Intestinal necrosis (RARE but DEADLY)
  • Sodium overload
🩺 Nursing:
  • Check bowel sounds
  • AVOID in ileus/obstruction
  • Monitor electrolytes
Albuterol
β2-Agonist
💡 Click to see details!
🎯 MOA:

Shifts K into cells (raises insulin)

📋 Use:

Adjunct hyperkalemia treatment

⚠️ Side Effects:
  • Tachycardia
  • Tremors
  • Anxiety
🩺 Nursing:
  • Monitor HR closely
  • Check K+ levels
  • NOT primary therapy - ADJUNCT only!

Question 1: A patient on Furosemide (Lasix) is complaining of muscle cramps and weakness. What lab value should you check FIRST?

A. Potassium level
B. Sodium level
C. Calcium level
D. Magnesium level
✅ Correct Answer: A
Rationale: Loop diuretics like Lasix cause hypoKALEMIA! Muscle cramps and weakness are classic signs of low potassium. Remember: "LOOP makes you LOSE potassium!" The patient needs potassium replacement and should eat potassium-rich foods like bananas.

Question 2: When should phosphate binders be administered?

A. With meals
B. On an empty stomach
C. At bedtime
D. One hour before meals
✅ Correct Answer: A
Rationale: ALL phosphate binders MUST be given WITH MEALS! They work by binding dietary phosphate in the GI tract to decrease absorption. This applies to calcium acetate, sevelamer, and lanthanum carbonate. Remember: "Phosphate Binders = Bind with FOOD!"

Question 3: A CKD patient is receiving Epoetin alfa (ESA). What is the TARGET hemoglobin level?

A. 12-14 g/dL (normal range)
B. 14-16 g/dL
C. 10-11 g/dL
D. 8-9 g/dL
✅ Correct Answer: C
Rationale: 🔥 HIGH-YIELD! Target Hgb is 10-11 g/dL, NOT normal range! Overcorrection increases the risk of stroke and MI. Also remember to check IRON stores before starting epoetin and monitor BP closely (hypertension is a major side effect).

Question 4: In a hyperkalemia emergency, which medication should be given FIRST to protect the heart?

A. Regular insulin
B. Calcium gluconate
C. Kayexalate
D. Albuterol
✅ Correct Answer: B
Rationale: Calcium gluconate stabilizes the cardiac membrane and protects against arrhythmias. It does NOT lower potassium - it just protects the heart! The order is: 1) Calcium gluconate (protect heart), 2) Insulin + D50 (shift K into cells), 3) Albuterol/bicarb (adjunct), 4) Kayexalate/dialysis (remove K).

Question 5: When giving Ferrous Sulfate (iron), what should you avoid administering it with?

A. Vitamin C
B. Orange juice
C. Water
D. Milk or antacids
✅ Correct Answer: D
Rationale: Do NOT give iron with milk or antacids - they decrease absorption! Instead, give with Vitamin C (orange juice) to INCREASE absorption. Also use a straw for liquid forms to prevent teeth staining, and teach patients that black stools are normal!

Question 6: What important lab should be drawn BEFORE administering Vancomycin?

A. Trough level
B. Peak level
C. Random level
D. Potassium level
✅ Correct Answer: A
Rationale: TROUGH levels are drawn BEFORE the dose! This ensures you're maintaining therapeutic levels without toxicity. Vancomycin is nephrotoxic and ototoxic, so monitor BUN/Cr closely and infuse slowly to prevent Red Man Syndrome.

Question 7: A patient on Cinacalcet develops muscle twitching and tingling around the mouth. What electrolyte imbalance is this?

A. Hyperkalemia
B. Hypocalcemia
C. Hypernatremia
D. Hypomagnesemia
✅ Correct Answer: B
Rationale: Cinacalcet lowers PTH but can cause HYPOcalcemia! Signs include tetany, tingling (especially perioral), muscle spasms, and seizures. Monitor calcium levels FREQUENTLY and teach patients the signs of hypocalcemia.

Question 8: What time of day should you administer Furosemide to prevent nocturia?

A. Bedtime
B. Afternoon
C. Morning
D. Doesn't matter
✅ Correct Answer: C
Rationale: Give diuretics in the MORNING to prevent nocturia (nighttime urination) and sleep disruption! This improves patient compliance and quality of life. Also monitor I&O, daily weights, and check potassium before giving.
