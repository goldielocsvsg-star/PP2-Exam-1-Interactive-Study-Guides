🫘 AKI & CKD Study Guide

Acute Kidney Injury & Chronic Kidney Disease

🎯 Big Picture: What is AKI?

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) is a sudden, rapid decline in kidney function (hours → days) resulting in failure to:

🗑️

Eliminate Waste

💧

Maintain Fluid & Electrolyte Balance

⚖️

Maintain Acid-Base Balance

⚠️ CRITICAL: AKI = high mortality even if reversible! Can be reversible, progress to CKD, or occur on top of CKD (worst prognosis).
💡 #1 Preventable Cause: Perfusion loss is the biggest trigger for AKI!

📏 KDIGO Definition (MUST MEMORIZE!)

AKI is present if ANY of these occur:

↑ Creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL

Within 48 hours

↑ Creatinine ≥1.5× baseline

Within 7 days

Urine Output <0.5 mL/kg/hr

For 6 hours

📌 KEY: Creatinine rises LATE → Urine output is an earlier clue!

📊 KDIGO Staging (Test Favorite!)

Stage Creatinine Urine Output
Stage 1 1.5–1.9× baseline OR ↑ ≥0.3 <0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6–12 hr
Stage 2 2.0–2.9× baseline <0.5 mL/kg/hr ≥12 hr
Stage 3 ≥3× baseline OR ≥4.0 mg/dL OR dialysis Anuria ≥12 hr OR <0.3 mL/kg/hr ≥24 hr
🚨 Remember: Longer oliguria/anuria = poorer recovery!

🔬 The 3 Types of AKI

1️⃣ Prerenal

PERFUSION PROBLEM

↓ Blood flow to kidneys

  • Dehydration
  • Shock / Sepsis
  • Burns
  • Hemorrhage
  • NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors

📌 Most common & most reversible!

2️⃣ Intrarenal

KIDNEY DAMAGE

Direct injury to kidney tissue

  • Nephrotoxins (contrast, antibiotics)
  • Glomerulonephritis
  • Acute tubular necrosis
  • Immune disorders (lupus)

📌 Tubular cell sloughing → debris → obstruction

3️⃣ Postrenal

OBSTRUCTION

Urine outflow blocked

  • BPH
  • Kidney stones
  • Tumors
  • Neurogenic bladder

📌 Bilateral obstruction required to cause AKI

⚠️ Systemic Complications (EXAM GOLD!)

🧪

Metabolic

  • Metabolic acidosis
  • Hyperkalemia (MOST LETHAL!)
  • Hypocalcemia
  • Hyponatremia
❤️

Cardiopulmonary

  • Pulmonary edema
  • Heart failure
  • Hypertension
  • Pericarditis
🧠

Neurologic

  • Confusion
  • Asterixis
  • Seizures
🦠

Immunity

  • Pneumonia
  • Sepsis
🍽️

GI & Nutrition

  • GI bleeding
  • Malnutrition
🩸

Hematologic

  • Bleeding
  • Anemia (later)

💊 Nephrotoxins (MUST KNOW!)

Aminoglycosides (gentamicin)
Vancomycin
NSAIDs
ACE inhibitors
Chemotherapy
Contrast dye
Metformin (hold with contrast)
⚠️ DANGER: Multiple nephrotoxins = exponential AKI risk!

✅ Good Sign: Polyuria = recovery starting!
⚠️ But watch for risks:
  • Hypovolemia
  • Electrolyte loss

Nursing focus: Replace fluids & monitor labs closely!

📊

Monitor I&O hourly

⚖️

💧

Maintain euvolemia

📉

💉

🚨

Report rising creatinine IMMEDIATELY

🎯 Big Picture: What is CKD?

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a progressive, irreversible loss of kidney function lasting >3 months.

End-Stage Kidney Disease (ESKD): When kidneys can no longer maintain life without kidney replacement therapy.
Azotemia

Accumulation of nitrogenous wastes (↑ BUN, creatinine)

Uremia

Azotemia WITH clinical symptoms

Uremic Syndrome

Multisystem manifestations of uremia

📊 CKD Stages (MUST MEMORIZE!)

1
GFR >90 + kidney damage
Focus: Risk reduction
2
GFR 60-89
Focus: Slow progression
3
GFR 30-59
Focus: Manage complications
4
GFR 15-29
Focus: Prepare for KRT
5
GFR <15
Focus: Dialysis or transplant
📌 Remember: Albumin = kidney DAMAGE | GFR = kidney FUNCTION

  • Paresthesias
  • Lethargy

💧 Fluid & Sodium Changes

Early CKD

  • Polyuria
  • Sodium loss
  • Risk: hyponatremia & dehydration

Late CKD

  • ↓ urine output
  • Sodium + water retention
  • → HTN, edema, HF, pulmonary edema
📌 Tricky: Serum sodium may appear "normal" due to dilution!

🦴 Calcium-Phosphorus-Vitamin D Cascade (TEST FAVORITE!)

↓ Phosphate excretion
⬇️
Hyperphosphatemia
⬇️
↓ Active Vitamin D
⬇️
↓ Calcium absorption → Hypocalcemia
⬇️
↑ PTH → Bone resorption
⬇️
RENAL OSTEODYSTROPHY
🚨 DANGER: Ca × Phos product >70 = systemic calcification risk!
Consequences

Bone pain, fractures, osteomalacia, tooth loss, metastatic calcifications

❤️ Cardiovascular Complications

💀 #1 Cause of Death in ESKD = Cardiac Disease!
Cardiorenal Syndrome

Heart ↔ kidney dysfunction amplify each other

Severe HTN

Resistant to treatment, RAAS overactivation

LV Hypertrophy

→ Heart failure

Uremic Pericarditis

→ Tamponade (EMERGENCY!)

💉 Vascular Access (NCLEX FAVORITES!)

🏆 AV Fistula (BEST!)

  • Best long-term option
  • Matures in 2–3 months
  • Thrill + Bruit present
  • Lowest infection rate

AV Graft

  • Synthetic material
  • Matures in 2–4 weeks
  • Higher infection risk than fistula

Central Venous Catheter

  • Temporary use
  • Highest infection risk
  • Used emergently

🚫 AV Fistula Arm Precautions:

  • No BP measurements
  • No IV insertions
  • No venipunctures
  • No tight clothing/jewelry
  • No sleeping on access arm

🚨 Peritoneal Dialysis Red Flag

☁️ CLOUDY EFFLUENT = PERITONITIS!
Priority Actions:
  1. Culture effluent
  2. Start antibiotics immediately

🫘 Kidney Transplant Key Points

⚠️ Remember: Transplant is NOT a cure!
  • Requires lifelong immunosuppression
  • Risks: rejection, infection, CV disease

⚖️ AKI vs CKD Comparison

Feature AKI 🚨 CKD 🔄
Onset Sudden (hours-days) Gradual (>3 months)
Reversible? Often YES NO (irreversible)
Nephron Loss 50–95% Progressive
Urine Output Often ↓ (oliguria) Variable
Anemia Usually NO (early) YES (↓ EPO)
Dialysis Temporary Often permanent
Kidney Size Normal or enlarged Small (atrophied)
