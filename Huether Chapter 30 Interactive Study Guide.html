Croup (Laryngotracheobronchitis)
The "Seal Bark" Disease
▼
👶 Peak: 6 months - 3 years
🦠 Cause
Usually PARAINFLUENZA virus
(Also RSV, rhinovirus, adenovirus, metapneumovirus)
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Subglottic edema (cricoid = narrowest area) →
↑ Airway resistance → ↑ Work of breathing → Can spiral to failure!
The Classic Croup Triad
🦭 Barking "seal-like" cough
🗣️ Hoarse voice
😤 Inspiratory STRIDOR
📋 Typical Course
Viral prodrome (runny nose, sore throat, low fever) →
Then the classic triad appears →
Mild cases resolve in ~48 hours
🚨 Signs of SEVERE Croup
• Stridor AT REST
• Deep retractions
• Agitation, Tachycardia
• Cyanosis
💊 Treatment
Mainstay: GLUCOCORTICOIDS (oral/inhaled) - reduces symptoms + admissions
Severe: Nebulized EPINEPHRINE (↓ edema + bronchodilation)
Very Severe: Heliox (helium-O₂) → ↓ airflow resistance
Rare: Intubation
Viral Croup Spasmodic (Recurrent) Croup
Viral prodrome present NO prodrome
Gradual onset ABRUPT nighttime onset
Young kids (6mo-3yr) Often OLDER kids
Single episode 2+ episodes
Just treat Investigate triggers (GERD, allergies)

Bacterial Tracheitis
"Toxic Croup" - Rare but DEADLY!
▼
⚠️ Life-Threatening!
Rare but can be FATAL - often follows viral URI
🦠 Common Organisms
Staph aureus (including MRSA)
Strep pyogenes, Moraxella, GABHS
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Edema + Copious PURULENT secretions + Sloughing →
Forms PSEUDOMEMBRANE → Obstruction (can perforate!)
🩺 Presentation
  • HIGH FEVER - Toxic appearance!
  • Stridor + Hoarse voice + Harsh cough
  • THICK PURULENT SECRETIONS
  • Tachypnea
💊 Treatment
• ANTIBIOTICS (broad spectrum)
• Often needs EMERGENT INTUBATION
🚑
Acute Epiglottitis
AIRWAY EMERGENCY!
▼
📊 Epidemiology
Incidence ↓ in children since Hib vaccine!
Now more common in adults
Current organisms: S. pneumoniae, S. aureus, Moraxella
Epiglottitis = The 4 D's + NO COUGH
Drooling
Dysphagia
Dystonia (muffled voice)
Distress (tripod position)
+ NO COUGH! ← Red flag!
🩺 Classic Presentation
  • HIGH fever, severe sore throat
  • Toxic/anxious appearance
  • DROOLING (can't swallow)
  • Muffled "hot potato" voice
  • Stridor
  • TRIPOD POSITION
🚫 What NOT To Do!
DO NOT examine throat with tongue depressor!
Can trigger LARYNGOSPASM/COLLAPSE!

⚠️ Can progress to DEATH within hours!
🔬 Diagnosis
Definitive: Direct laryngoscopy (in controlled setting!)
X-ray may show swollen epiglottis but don't delay airway safety
💊 Treatment
• Keep child CALM - minimize agitation!
• Secure airway if severe obstruction
• Broad-spectrum antibiotics + Corticosteroids
🍬
Tonsillitis & Peritonsillar Abscess
Throat Infections Gone Wrong
▼
🔴 Tonsillitis
Mostly VIRAL (mono common!) but can be bacterial (GABHS, MRSA)
Severe obstruction → Corticosteroids
Bacterial → Antibiotics
Recurrent → Tonsillectomy
🎯 Peritonsillar Abscess
Pus between tonsillar capsule and pharyngeal muscle
Usually UNILATERAL - complication of tonsillitis
⚠️ Complications of Abscess
• Upper airway obstruction
• Spread to mediastinum
• Jugular vein obstruction
• Rupture → Aspiration!
🩺 Abscess Presentation
  • Fever, sore throat, dysphagia, drooling
  • TRISMUS (locked jaw!)
  • "Hot potato" muffled voice
  • Unilateral swelling + UVULA DEVIATION
💊 Treatment
Needle aspiration + Empiric antibiotics + Steroids
Sometimes I&D or tonsillectomy

Laryngomalacia & Tracheomalacia
Floppy Airways
▼
Feature Laryngomalacia Tracheomalacia
What? Soft LARYNGEAL cartilage Flaccid TRACHEAL cartilage
Collapse On INSPIRATION On EXPIRATION
Key Point #1 cause of chronic stridor in infants! Primary or secondary (vascular compression)
Diagnosis Laryngoscopy Bronchoscopy
Prognosis Most resolve by 1 year May need surgery if severe

Foreign Body Aspiration
Wrong Pipe! Peak: 1-3 Years
▼
👶 Peak: 1-3 years (exploring everything!)
⚠️ BIG RULE
Symptoms can BRIEFLY RESOLVE → Don't miss occult aspiration!
Acute Obstruction Occult Aspiration
Immediate symptoms Days to MONTHS later
Cough, stridor, hoarseness Chronic cough/wheeze
Can't speak, distress Recurrent pneumonia
Severe → Life-saving maneuvers! Atelectasis, abscess
📍 Location
Often RIGHT lower lobe bronchus (more vertical anatomy!)
X-ray shows air trapping/collapse
💊 Treatment
Bronchoscopic removal ± Antibiotics
😴
Pediatric Obstructive Sleep Apnea
Sleep-Disordered Breathing
▼
📊 Stats
Prevalence ~1-4% of children
Higher risk: Obesity, Black/Hispanic, Preterm, Family history
🎯 #1 Cause
ADENOTONSILLAR HYPERTROPHY
Also: Allergic rhinitis, craniofacial abnormalities, Down syndrome, obesity
🩺 Manifestations
Sleep: Snoring, labored breathing, gasping, pauses
Daytime: Sleepiness, behavior issues, poor school performance
Sometimes: Enuresis (bed-wetting)
🔬 Evaluation
Screen ALL children for snoring!
Gold standard: POLYSOMNOGRAPHY (sleep study)
💊 Treatment
If tonsillar cause: T&A (tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy)
If persistent/obesity: PAP therapy
Treat to prevent: HTN, CV disease, learning problems
🫧
Surfactant Deficiency Disorder (RDS)
The Preemie Problem
▼
👶 Almost exclusively PREMATURE infants!
🎯 Risk Factors
  • Prematurity (<28 weeks = highest risk!)
  • Low birth weight, Male sex
  • C-section without labor
  • Maternal diabetes
  • Perinatal asphyxia
Pathophysiology Chain Reaction
No surfactant → ↑ Surface tension →
Alveoli COLLAPSE at end-expiration →
↓ Gas exchange → HYPOXEMIA →
Hard to reopen → ↑ Work of breathing → HYPERCAPNIA →
Pulmonary vasoconstriction → May revert to fetal circulation!
🩺 Presentation (Minutes After Birth!)
  • Tachypnea
  • GRUNTING
  • Retractions, Nasal flaring
  • Cyanosis
  • Hypoxemia REFRACTORY to O₂!
Peaks ~3 days; Many recover in 10-14 days
💊 Treatment
Prevention: ANTENATAL STEROIDS (accelerate lung maturity!)
Supportive: O₂ + Lung-protective ventilation
Key: EXOGENOUS SURFACTANT early (15-30 min)
Watch for → BPD risk!
🫁
Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia (BPD)
Chronic Lung Disease of Prematurity
▼
👶 Smallest preemies: 23-28 weeks
⚙️ What Is It?
Chronic inflammatory lung disease from:
• Arrested lung development
• Mechanical ventilation injury
• Oxygen toxicity
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Ventilation + O₂ toxicity + Immature lungs →
Inflammatory injury (neutrophils, macrophages) →
Abnormal repair → Impaired alveolar development →
Atelectasis, edema, fibrosis, pulmonary HTN
🩺 Manifestations
  • Chronic hypoxemia/hypercapnia
  • ↑ Work of breathing
  • Feeding difficulty
  • Bronchospasm, mucus plugging
  • Pulmonary hypertension
  • "Dusky spells" with agitation/feeding
💊 Treatment
• Prolonged respiratory support (CPAP/nasal vent)
• Diuretics, Low-dose steroids, Caffeine citrate
• Bronchodilators for bronchospasm
• Vasodilators if severe PH
• Vitamin A + Nutrition
• Monitor into ADULTHOOD!
🦠
Bronchiolitis
RSV's Favorite Target
▼
👶 Mainly <2 years
🦠 Causes
#1: RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus)
Also: Rhinovirus, Adenovirus, Influenza, Metapneumovirus
⚠️ High-Risk Infants
Prematurity, BPD, Congenital heart disease, Immunodeficiency
⚙️ Pathophysiology
RSV starts as URI → Lower infection →
Necrosis of epithelium + Loss of cilia →
Inflammation + Edema + Mucus plugs + Bronchospasm →
Narrowed small airways →
Worse on EXPIRATION → Air trapping → ↑ Work of breathing
🩺 Presentation
Starts: Runny nose + Cough →
Then: Fever, Poor feeding, Lethargy →
Progresses: Tachypnea, WHEEZE, Retractions

⚠️ Young infants can present with APNEA FIRST!
🚫 What NOT To Give Routinely!
❌ Bronchodilators
❌ Steroids
❌ Antibiotics
These are NOT recommended routinely!
💊 Treatment = SUPPORTIVE!
• High-flow O₂ / Noninvasive ventilation if needed
• Hand hygiene, Avoid smoke
• Breastfeeding (protective!)
• Prevention: NIRSEVIMAB for high-risk infants
🫁
Pediatric Pneumonia
CAP vs HAP/VAP
▼
Type Cause Key Points
Viral (Most Common!) RSV, Rhinovirus, Influenza Supportive care
Bacterial CAP S. pneumoniae (#1) Often follows viral URI
Atypical Mycoplasma (school-age) Gradual onset, dry cough
HAP/VAP Resistant organisms Worse outcomes
🩺 Presentation
Dyspnea, Fever, Cough, Crackles, Wheeze
Tachypnea, Retractions, Nasal flaring

Bacterial clue: "Viral then SUDDENLY WORSE" + High fever + Toxic
🔬 Evaluation
Often clinical diagnosis!
CXR not needed outpatient unless severe/no improvement in 48h
Pulse ox helps assess severity
💊 Treatment
Supportive: O₂, Hydration, Nutrition
CAP: Single empiric antibiotic (age-based)
HAP: Broad-spectrum → Narrow when pathogen ID'd
Prevention: Vaccines! (Flu, RSV, Pneumococcal)

Aspiration Pneumonitis
Wrong Pipe Consequences
▼
📌 What Gets Aspirated?
Meconium, Food, Saliva/Gastric contents, Hydrocarbons
⚙️ Severity Depends On
• Volume of aspirate
• pH (very low/high = worse!)
• Bacteria present
⚠️ Chronic Aspiration
Kids with neurologic impairment + dysphagia →
Recurrent pneumonia → Bronchiectasis → Fibrosis → Failure
Acute Chronic
Sudden cough, dyspnea, distress Cough/wheeze + Recurrent pneumonias
💊 Treatment
• Antibiotics (often polymicrobial)
• Swallow/feeding therapy
• Anticholinergics for excessive secretions
• Botulinum toxin A salivary gland injection
• Sometimes tracheostomy
🌊
Pediatric ARDS (PARDS)
Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome
▼
📊 Mortality ~24%!
Worse with: Immunocompromise, Vasopressors, Multi-organ dysfunction
🎯 Causes
Direct: Pneumonia, Aspiration, Near-drowning, Smoke inhalation
Indirect: Sepsis, Pancreatitis, Trauma
Some adolescent cases linked to VAPING!
📋 Diagnostic Criteria
  • Onset within 7 days of insult
  • NOT explained by heart failure
  • New BILATERAL opacities on imaging
  • REFRACTORY HYPOXEMIA
💊 Treatment
• Supportive: Maintain oxygenation, minimize lung injury
• High-flow nasal cannula ± Positive pressure
• Lung-protective mechanical ventilation
• ECMO if failure of lung-protective strategies
• Prone positioning can help
• Steroids/Surfactant NOT routinely recommended

Childhood Asthma
~6.5% of U.S. Children
▼
📌 Definition
Chronic inflammatory airway disease with:
• Variable symptoms
• Variable expiratory airflow limitation
• REVERSIBLE!
🎯 Risk Factors
  • Genetic susceptibility
  • Environmental exposures (allergens, smoke, pollution)
  • Early viral wheezing (RSV/rhinovirus) = STRONG risk!
  • Dysbiosis, Prematurity, C-section, Antibiotics
  • Obesity (↑ inflammation)
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Often Th2-driven Type I hypersensitivity →
IgE, Mast cells, Eosinophils →
Bronchospasm + Mucus + Edema →
Expiratory obstruction → Air trapping → V/Q mismatch → Hypoxemia
🩺 Acute Attack
  • Cough, Expiratory WHEEZE, SOB
  • Tachypnea, Tachycardia
  • Retractions, Nasal flaring
  • Infants may "HEAD BOB"
  • Pulsus paradoxus may appear
⚠️ OMINOUS SIGN!
FAINT breath sounds = "Tight" chest
Poor air movement → Imminent failure!
💊 Treatment
Education + Trigger control!
Persistent asthma: Start with LOW-DOSE inhaled corticosteroid
Before stepping up: Check technique, environment, comorbidities
Step down only after ≥3 months control
🧬
Cystic Fibrosis (CF)
CFTR Mutation - The Thick Mucus Disease
▼
🧬 Genetics
Autosomal RECESSIVE
CFTR gene mutation (chromosome 7) →
Defective chloride transport →
THICK, DEHYDRATED MUCUS
CF Lung Vicious Cycle
Thick mucus → Plugging + Impaired clearance →
Chronic INFECTION →
Chronic neutrophilic INFLAMMATION →
BRONCHIECTASIS →
Respiratory FAILURE
⚠️ High-Yield Complications
  • Recurrent infections/pneumonia
  • Bronchiectasis, Cysts, Bullae → Pneumothorax risk!
  • Hemoptysis (enlarged bronchial arteries)
  • Pulmonary HTN → Cor pulmonale (late)
  • CF-related DIABETES (worsens outcomes)
🩺 Clinical Features
  • Persistent cough/wheeze, Heavy sputum
  • Recurrent pneumonia
  • Barrel chest, CLUBBING
  • GI: Malabsorption, obstruction
🔬 Diagnosis
• Positive newborn screen + Confirmatory testing
• SWEAT CHLORIDE TEST (chloride elevated!)
• Genetic testing for CFTR variants
💊 Treatment (Game Changer!)
CFTR MODULATORS - Major breakthrough! (esp. F508del)
• Airway clearance (Chest PT devices)
• Bronchodilators
• Mucolytics (Dornase alfa)
• Antibiotics (inhaled/oral/IV)
• Nutrition support
• Lung transplant (end-stage)
• Gene therapy emerging!

Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS)
Prevention is KEY!
▼
👶 Peak: 2-4 months
📌 Definition
Sudden unexplained death <12 months that remains unexplained after:
• Autopsy
• Scene investigation
• History review
(Excludes confirmed suffocation/strangulation)
⚠️ Risk Factors
  • Prone/side sleeping
  • Soft bedding, Overheating
  • Maternal/secondhand SMOKING
  • Prematurity, Low birth weight, Male
  • Family history (sibling)
  • Young maternal age, Substance use
🧠 Leading Theories
• Brainstem/serotonin abnormalities → Impaired arousal
• Autonomic dysregulation
• Immune/airway inflammation
• Possible infection triggers
