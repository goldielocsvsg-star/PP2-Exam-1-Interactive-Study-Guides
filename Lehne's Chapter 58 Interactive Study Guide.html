Pharmacology Study Guide | Iron • B12 • Folic Acid

🩸 Stages of RBC Development

Know these IN ORDER (bone marrow → blood):

1. Proerythroblast

📍 In bone marrow | ❌ NO hemoglobin yet

2. Erythroblast

📍 In bone marrow | ✅ HAS hemoglobin

3. Reticulocyte

📍 In blood | 🔄 Immature RBC, still has some RNA

4. Erythrocyte

📍 In blood | ✨ Mature RBC, full hemoglobin

🔴 Iron: What Does It Do?
Hemoglobin (70-80%)

In RBCs - carries O₂ throughout body

Myoglobin (10%)

In muscles - stores O₂ for muscle use

Enzymes

Various metabolic reactions

🔄 Iron Movement in the Body
1️⃣ Absorption (Small Intestine) +
  • Absorbs 5-20% of dietary iron
  • Fe²⁺ (ferrous) absorbed BETTER than Fe³⁺
  • ✅ Vitamin C ↑ absorption
  • ❌ Food ↓ absorption
2️⃣ After Absorption - Two Options +
  • 📦 STORED in mucosal cells as FERRITIN
  • 🚚 TRANSPORTED in blood bound to TRANSFERRIN
3️⃣ Where Does Transferrin-Bound Iron Go? +
  • 🦴 Bone marrow → Hemoglobin production
  • 🫀 Liver & tissues → Ferritin (storage)
  • 💪 Muscle/other tissues → Myoglobin & enzymes
4️⃣ Recycling & Elimination +

♻️ Recycling:

  • RBCs live ~120 days, then broken down
  • Iron is REUSED and sent back on transferrin

🚮 Elimination (very little normally):

  • ~1 mg/day lost via: sloughing GI mucosa, bile, urine, sweat
  • ⚠️ BLOOD LOSS = MAJOR way to lose iron!

💡 Memory Trick for Iron Storage vs Transport

Ferritin = iron is "Sittin'" (storage)
Transferrin = "Trans"port (moving iron)
💊 Oral Iron Therapy
⭐ FERROUS SULFATE = DRUG OF CHOICE

Other ferrous salts (gluconate, fumarate, aspartate) are equally effective - differ in % elemental iron

✅ Indications

  • TREATMENT of iron deficiency anemia
  • PREVENTION in: pregnancy, chronic blood loss, high-risk groups

⚠️ Major Adverse Effects

  • GI disturbances (dose-dependent): nausea, heartburn, bloating, constipation, diarrhea
  • BLACK/GREEN STOOLS - harmless, NOT GI bleed!
  • Teeth staining (liquid prep) - dilute, use straw, rinse mouth

💊 Dosing Principles

  • Typical: ~65 mg elemental iron TID (~200 mg/day total)
  • BETWEEN MEALS = best absorption (but ↑ GI upset)
  • Space doses evenly throughout day
  • Duration: 1-3 months to correct anemia + 4-6 months to replenish stores
⚠️
Drug Interactions:
• Antacids ↓ iron absorption
• Tetracyclines ↔ ↓ absorption of BOTH
• Vitamin C ↑ absorption BUT also ↑ GI side effects

💡 Why is Carbonyl Iron Special?

✅ Pure elemental iron (microparticles)
✅ Same efficacy as ferrous salts
✅ Slower absorption → ↓ toxicity risk
✅ SAFER if accidentally ingested by children

💉 Parenteral Iron (Iron Dextran)
📌
Use ONLY when oral iron cannot work:
• Cannot be absorbed (intestinal disease)
• Blood loss too great for oral to keep up (500-1000 mL/week)
• Oral intolerable or worsens GI disease
🚨
BIG SAFETY ALERT - RISK OF FATAL ANAPHYLAXIS!
Must:
• Give a TEST DOSE before full dose
• Have EPINEPHRINE & resuscitation equipment ready
• Monitor after BOTH test dose and full dose

Other Parenteral Products (know they exist):

Ferric carboxymaltose Iron sucrose Sodium ferric gluconate Ferumoxytol Ferric pyrophosphate citrate
✨
Priority: ORAL iron > IV iron > IM iron (IV preferred over IM)
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Implications for Iron
🔍 Assessment +
  • Fatigue, pallor, dyspnea, tachycardia, chest pain
  • Menstrual history, GI bleed, diet, pregnancy
🔬 Labs to Monitor +
  • Hgb/Hct
  • RBC indices (microcytic, hypochromic)
  • Serum iron, ferritin, TIBC
📚 Patient Teaching +
  • Take BETWEEN MEALS if possible
  • Expect DARK STOOLS (normal!)
  • Liquids: dilute, use straw, rinse mouth
  • Avoid antacids and tetracyclines near dose
  • Don't switch brands without checking dose
  • ⚠️ KEEP AWAY FROM CHILDREN!
📈 Monitoring Response +
  • Reticulocytes ↑ in 4-7 days
  • Hgb/Hct start ↑ in 1 week
  • Hgb ↑ by ≥2 g/dL in 1 month = good response!
⚠️ Iron Toxicity (HIGH-YIELD PEDIATRICS!)
☠️
Leading cause of FATAL POISONING in children!
Lethal dose in kids: 2-10 g elemental iron
⏰ Early Signs/Symptoms
  • 🤢 Nausea, vomiting
  • 💩 Diarrhea
  • ⚡ Shock
⏰ Later Signs/Symptoms
  • ⚗️ Metabolic acidosis
  • 🫁 Gastric necrosis
  • 🫀 Hepatic failure
  • 💨 Pulmonary edema
  • 💔 Vasomotor collapse

🏥 Treatment of Iron Toxicity:

1. Check serum iron

2. X-ray for tablets

Iron tablets are radiopaque

3. Whole-bowel irrigation

PEG solution to flush out tablets

4. If iron very high → DEFEROXAMINE IV

Iron CHELATOR (binds and removes iron)

💊
Iron Chelators to Know:
• Deferoxamine - IV for acute toxicity
• Deferasirox, Deferiprone - for chronic iron overload

💜 Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin)
🔑
Main Function: Needed for DNA SYNTHESIS → affects ALL rapidly dividing cells!
🔬 How B12 Works & Moves +
  • Works by activating folic acid → active folate supports DNA synthesis
  • Requires:
    • Intrinsic factor (from parietal cells in stomach)
    • Ileum for absorption
    • Transcobalamin II for transport
  • Stored mostly in LIVER (2-3 mg total)
  • Very slow excretion → deficiency takes YEARS to appear
  • Daily adult need: ~2.4 µg/day
🎯 Causes of B12 Deficiency
⭐
Most are from MALABSORPTION, NOT diet!
Lack of Intrinsic Factor → PERNICIOUS ANEMIA
  • Atrophy of parietal cells
  • Total gastrectomy
Intestinal Disease
  • Regional enteritis
  • Celiac disease
Other Causes
  • ↓ stomach acid (age, PPIs)
  • Antibodies vs IF-B12 complex
⚠️ Consequences of B12 Deficiency (HIGH-YIELD!)
1. Megaloblastic Anemia
  • Big, immature RBCs (megaloblasts)
  • Caused by impaired DNA synthesis
  • Severe → hypoxia, HF, dysrhythmias
2. NEUROLOGIC DAMAGE 🧠
  • Demyelination of neurons
  • Early: paresthesias, ↓ reflexes
  • Late: memory loss, mood changes, psychosis
  • ⚠️ May be IRREVERSIBLE!
3. Other Effects
  • Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia
  • GI disturbances, glossitis
  • Mouth ulcers
💊 Treatment: Cyanocobalamin
⭐ DRUG OF CHOICE for ALL forms of B12 deficiency

⚠️ Adverse Effects

  • Generally SAFE
  • Watch for HYPOKALEMIA - new RBCs take up potassium!

💉 Routes of Administration

1. ORAL
  • Works for MOST patients
  • Even with malabsorption if dose is HIGH
  • Typical: 1000-10,000 µg/day
2. PARENTERAL (IM or deep subQ)
  • ❌ NOT IV!
  • Use when: severe neuro involvement, can't rely on oral
  • 100 µg monthly FOR LIFE if irreversible cause
3. INTRANASAL (Nascobal)
  • 500 µg in ONE nostril ONCE weekly
  • ❌ Not if nasal issues (URI, congestion)
  • No hot foods/drinks 1 hr before/after
⚠️ CRITICAL WARNING: B12 + Folic Acid Interaction
🚨
VERY TESTABLE!

Large doses of FOLIC ACID can:
✅ CORRECT the anemia
❌ NOT fix the neurologic damage

This MASKS B12 deficiency → neuro damage continues silently!
⭐
NEVER treat B12 deficiency with folic acid alone!
If using both, ensure B12 dose is ADEQUATE first!
💚 Folic Acid (Folate)
🔑
Main Function: Essential for DNA SYNTHESIS (like B12)
🔬 How Folate Works +
  • Must be converted to ACTIVE form
  • Usually via B12-dependent pathway
  • At high doses, can use B12-independent pathway
  • Absorbed in upper small intestine
  • Undergoes enterohepatic recirculation
  • NOT conserved as tightly as B12 → deficiency develops QUICKLY
📊 Daily Folate Requirements
Population Daily Need Notes
Adults (both sexes) 400 µg/day Baseline
Pregnancy 600 µg/day ⚠️ Critical for fetal development!
Lactation 500 µg/day Passed in breast milk
People who may become pregnant 400-800 µg/day SUPPLEMENTAL (in addition to diet)
Malabsorption (sprue) Up to 2000 µg/day Higher doses needed
🎯 Causes of Folate Deficiency
1. Poor Diet (Most Common)
  • Especially in ALCOHOL USE DISORDER
  • Alcohol ↓ intake AND messes up enterohepatic recirculation
2. Malabsorption
  • Sprue
  • Other intestinal diseases
3. Some Drugs
  • Methotrexate
  • Phenytoin
  • (Less common cause)
⚠️ Consequences of Folate Deficiency
⭐
Similar to B12 deficiency BUT NO NEUROLOGIC INJURY in adults!
In Adults
  • Megaloblastic anemia
  • Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia
  • Glossitis, GI mucosal injury
  • ❌ NO neuro symptoms!
In FETUS (Critical!)
  • NEURAL TUBE DEFECTS:
  • Spina bifida
  • Anencephaly
  • ⚠️ Why pregnant women MUST supplement!
💊 Folic Acid Treatment
Folic Acid Preparations

📋 Forms Available

  • Inactive form (most common): Folic acid, folacin, folate
  • Active form: Leucovorin calcium, folinic acid (mainly for chemo regimens)

✅ Indications

  • Treatment of megaloblastic anemia (folate deficiency)
  • PROPHYLAXIS in pregnancy & lactation
  • Initial treatment with B12 in severe megaloblastic anemia

⚠️ Adverse Effects

  • Oral folate is VERY SAFE
  • Main concern: MASKING B12 deficiency!

💊 Routes & Doses

  • ORAL preferred
  • Available tablets: 0.4, 0.8, 1 mg
  • Treatment: 1000-2000 µg/day × 1-2 weeks → then 400 µg/day maintenance
  • Injection only if severe GI malabsorption
📈
Monitoring Response:
• Megaloblasts disappear in ~48 hours
• Reticulocyte count ↑ in 2-3 days
• Hct rises by week 2

"Folate affects the Fetus" - Folate deficiency = Neural tube defects
