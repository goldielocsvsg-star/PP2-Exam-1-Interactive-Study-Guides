Dyspnea
The #1 Most Common Symptom!
▼
📌 Definition
Subjective sensation of difficult or uncomfortable breathing - "air hunger"
🧠 Mechanisms
  • CNS respiratory centers
  • Chemoreceptors (central & peripheral)
  • Lung sensory receptors
  • Chest wall mechanoreceptors
  • Psychological influences
Memory Trick: Types of Dyspnea
D.O.P. = DOE (exertion) → Orthopnea (lying flat) → PND (nighttime)
They progress in severity! DOE is the EARLIEST sign!
Type When? Why?
DOE With activity Earliest sign!
Orthopnea Lying flat ↑ pulmonary blood volume
PND Sleep → wakes up Delayed orthopnea
🚨 RED FLAGS - Severe Dyspnea
  • Accessory muscle use
  • Tripod positioning
  • Nasal flaring
  • Retractions

Cough
Protective Reflex Extraordinaire
▼
⚙️ The Cough Sequence
1️⃣ Inspiration → 2️⃣ Glottis closes → 3️⃣ Forceful muscle contraction → 4️⃣ Glottis opens → 💨 BOOM!
Acute (<3 weeks) Chronic (>8 weeks) Treatments
URIs, pneumonia Postnasal drip Central: Dextromethorphan
CHF Asthma Peripheral: Benzonatate
Aspiration GERD, ACE inhibitors Refractory: Opioids

Hemoptysis & Sputum
What Your Lungs Are Telling You
▼
🎨 Sputum Color Guide
  • Clear/foamy → Pulmonary edema
  • Yellow/green (purulent) → Infection
  • Bloody → Hemoptysis
🩸 Hemoptysis = Coughing up BLOOD
Top Causes: Lung cancer, Bronchiectasis, PE, Pneumonia

Key: Differentiate from hematemesis (vomiting blood from GI!)

Cyanosis & Clubbing
Visual Signs of Hypoxemia
▼
Type Location Cause
Peripheral Cyanosis Fingers, toes ↓ circulation (cold, HF)
Central Cyanosis Lips, tongue ↑ deoxygenated Hgb (hypoxemia)
⚠️ Important!
Cyanosis appears LATE - unreliable indicator!
Anemia & CO poisoning → hypoxia WITHOUT cyanosis!
🖐️ Clubbing
What: Bulbous enlargement of distal digits
Causes: Lung cancer (#1), Chronic hypoxemia, CF, CHD
Mechanism: PDGF & VEGF → connective tissue growth

Breathing Patterns
From Normal to Not-So-Normal
▼
✅ Normal (Eupnea)
RR: 8-16/min | VT: 400-800 mL | Effortless & rhythmic
Pattern Description Think...
Kussmaul Deep, rapid Metabolic acidosis (DKA!)
Cheyne-Stokes Cyclic apnea/hyperpnea ↓ brainstem perfusion
Gasping Irregular, desperate Shock, cerebral hypoxia
Hypo vs Hyper - ventilation
HYPOventilation = ↓ breathing = ↑ CO₂ = Respiratory ACIDOSIS
HYPERventilation = ↑ breathing = ↓ CO₂ = Respiratory ALKALOSIS

Hypercapnia
Too Much CO₂!
▼
📊 Definition
PaCO₂ >44 mm Hg → Respiratory ACIDOSIS
🎯 Causes
  • ↓ respiratory drive (drugs, CNS injury)
  • Neuromuscular disease
  • Chest wall abnormalities
  • Airway obstruction (sleep apnea, tumors)
  • ↑ dead space (emphysema)
⚡ Effects
Cerebral vasodilation → Somnolence/Coma
Dysrhythmias
Secondary hypoxemia
📉
Hypoxemia
Not Enough O₂!
▼
📊 Definition
↓ PaO₂ (arterial oxygen)
The 4 Mechanisms of Hypoxemia
1️⃣ ↓ Alveolar O₂ (↓ FiO₂, hypoventilation)
2️⃣ Diffusion impairment
3️⃣ V/Q MISMATCH - MOST COMMON! ⭐
4️⃣ ↓ Pulmonary perfusion (shunts)
Low V/Q High V/Q Compensation
= Shunting = Dead space Hypoxic vasoconstriction
Edema, Atelectasis PE, Emphysema Can → Pulmonary HTN!
🚨
Acute Respiratory Failure
Emergency Time!
▼
📋 Diagnostic Criteria
• PaO₂ ≤60 mm Hg
• PaCO₂ ≥50 mm Hg with pH ≤7.25
Type Problem Notes
Hypoxemic ↓ O₂ Type I
Hypercapnic ↑ CO₂ Type II
Mixed Both! MOST COMMON ⭐
🏥 Management
  • Oxygen therapy
  • Ventilatory support
  • Prevent complications (atelectasis, pneumonia)

Pneumothorax
Air Where It Shouldn't Be!
▼
📌 Definition
Air in pleural space → Lung collapse!
Type Cause Features
Primary Spontaneous Blebs rupture Young, tall, thin males
Secondary Spontaneous Lung disease COPD patients
Traumatic/Iatrogenic Injury/procedures Post-line placement
🆘 TENSION PNEUMOTHORAX = EMERGENCY!
One-way valve: Air IN but can't get OUT
→ Mediastinal shift
→ Severe hypoxemia + HYPOTENSION

Tx: Immediate needle decompression → Chest tube
💊 Treatment
• Chest tube drainage
• Pleurodesis if recurrent

Pleural Effusion
Fluid in the Pleural Space
▼
Type Cause Characteristics
Transudative CHF, Cirrhosis Low protein, low cells
Exudative Infection, Cancer High protein, high cells
Empyema Infected effusion PUS in pleural space!
Hemothorax Trauma, Surgery BLOOD in pleural space
Chylothorax Thoracic duct injury Milky lymphatic fluid
🩺 Signs & Symptoms
  • Dyspnea
  • Dullness to percussion
  • ↓ breath sounds
💊 Treatment
• Treat underlying cause
• Thoracentesis or chest tube if large

Chest Wall Restriction
When the Cage Can't Expand
▼
⚙️ Effect
↓ Tidal volume → Hypoventilation
🎯 Causes
  • Kyphoscoliosis
  • Ankylosing spondylitis
  • Flail chest (paradoxical movement!)
  • Neuromuscular disease
💊 Management
• ↓ FVC on PFTs
• Treat cause, pain control
• Incentive spirometry
• Ventilation if severe
🔒 What is Restrictive Disease?
STIFF LUNGS that can't expand properly!
↓ Lung compliance → ↓ Total lung capacity

Atelectasis
Collapsed Alveoli
▼
📌 Definition
Collapse of alveoli/lung tissue → V/Q mismatch → HYPOXEMIA
Type Mechanism Example
Obstructive Airway blocked Mucus plug, tumor
Compression External pressure Effusion, tumor
Surfactant impairment ↓ surfactant ARDS, RDS
🛡️ Prevention
  • Incentive spirometry
  • Early ambulation
  • Deep breathing exercises

Pulmonary Edema & ARDS
Fluid Overload!
▼
Feature Cardiogenic Noncardiogenic (ARDS)
Cause Heart failure Diffuse alveolar injury
PCWP HIGH (>18) Normal
BNP Elevated Normal
🌊 ARDS Phases
1️⃣ Exudative - Injury & inflammation
2️⃣ Proliferative - Repair begins
3️⃣ Fibrotic - Scarring
🆘 ARDS Hallmarks
• REFRACTORY HYPOXEMIA (doesn't respond to O₂!)
• ↓ Compliance
• Bilateral infiltrates ("white out" on CXR)
💨 What is Obstructive Disease?
AIR TRAPPING - Air can get IN but can't get OUT!
↑ Residual volume → ↑ Total lung capacity

Asthma
Reversible Obstruction
▼
📌 Key Features
• REVERSIBLE airflow obstruction
• Type I hypersensitivity
• Bronchial hyperresponsiveness
🎭 Two-Phase Response
Early Phase: Minutes - Bronchoconstriction, histamine release
Late Phase: Hours - Inflammation, eosinophils
🩺 Clinical Features
  • Wheezing
  • Dyspnea
  • Chest tightness
  • Cough (especially at night)
🆘 Status Asthmaticus = EMERGENCY!
Severe asthma NOT responding to treatment
⚠️ SILENT CHEST = OMINOUS SIGN!
(No air movement = imminent respiratory failure)

COPD
Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease
▼
📌 Definition
Progressive, IRREVERSIBLE airflow limitation
(vs. Asthma which is reversible!)
Phenotype Features Nickname
Chronic Bronchitis Productive cough ≥3mo/yr x 2yrs "Blue Bloater"
Emphysema Alveolar wall destruction, air trapping "Pink Puffer"
α1-Antitrypsin Deficiency Young, lower lobe emphysema Genetic!
COPD Hallmarks
A.H.H.C.
Air trapping
Hypercapnia
Hypoxemia
Cor pulmonale (right heart failure)
🦠 Bronchiectasis
Permanent bronchial dilation
• Chronic productive cough
• COPIOUS purulent sputum
• Hemoptysis, Clubbing
Tx: Antibiotics, Chest PT, Bronchodilators

▼

Tuberculosis (TB)
The Great Masquerader
▼
📊 Stats
• #2 infectious killer worldwide
• 10.6 million cases/year
• ~13 million Americans with LATENT TB
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Airborne transmission → Macrophages can't kill it →
GRANULOMA (tubercle) formation →
Caseation necrosis → LATENT TB →
Can reactivate with immunosuppression!
Latent TB Active TB Testing
Asymptomatic Night sweats PPD (TST)
Not contagious Weight loss, Fever IGRA (preferred!)
Contained in granulomas Hemoptysis, Cough CXR, Sputum culture
💊 Treatment
BCG vaccine: Only TB vaccine!
LTBI: Prophylactic therapy for high-risk
Active TB: Respiratory isolation + 4-month regimen

Lung Abscess
Pus Pocket in the Lung
▼
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Aspiration (alcohol, seizures, anesthesia) →
Liquefactive necrosis → Abscess fills with pus →
Drains into bronchus → CAVITATION
🩺 Classic Presentation
MALODOROUS SPUTUM! (Think: smells bad = anaerobes!)
+ Fever, Chills, Cough, Pleuritic chest pain
💊 Treatment
• Prolonged antibiotics
• Chest physiotherapy
• Bronchoscopy drainage if needed
🩸
Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
Clot in the Lungs!
▼
📊 Stats
• 370,000 VTE cases/year in US
• High mortality - sudden death common!
• COVID-19 ↑ VTE risk significantly
Virchow's Triad (Risk Factors)
S.E.H.
Stasis (venous)
Endothelial injury
Hypercoagulability
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Thrombus (usually from DVT) → Lodges in pulmonary vessels →
Releases serotonin, histamine, endothelin →
Vasoconstriction + V/Q mismatch + Hypoxemia
Massive PE → Shock, RV failure, DEATH
🩺 Clinical Presentation
• SUDDEN dyspnea
• Pleuritic chest pain
• Tachypnea, Tachycardia
• Anxiety, Hemoptysis
• Syncope (large PE!)
🔬 Workup
• D-dimer (rule out)
• CT pulmonary angiography (gold standard)
• ABGs: Hypoxemia + Respiratory ALKALOSIS
• BNP/Troponin for RV strain
💊 Treatment
Primary: Anticoagulation (LMWH, DOACs)
Severe: Hospitalize + O₂
Unstable: Thrombolytics (tPA) or Thrombectomy
Contraindications: IVC filter
📈
Pulmonary Hypertension
High Pressure in the Lungs
▼
📊 Definition
Mean pulmonary artery pressure >20 mm Hg
🎯 Causes
  • PAH (idiopathic, heritable, drugs)
  • Left heart disease (MOST COMMON!)
  • Lung disease/hypoxia
  • Chronic thromboembolic disease
⚙️ Pathophysiology
Endothelial dysfunction →
↑ Vasoconstrictors (endothelin) + ↓ Vasodilators (NO, prostacyclin) →
Vascular remodeling → RV hypertrophy → RV FAILURE
🩺 Clinical Features
Fatigue, DOE, Chest discomfort
Peripheral edema, JVD, Loud P2
💊 Treatment
O₂, Diuretics, Anticoagulation
Vasodilators: CCBs, Prostacyclins, PDE-5 inhibitors, Endothelin antagonists
Avoid: Pregnancy, tobacco, NSAIDs
❤️
Cor Pulmonale
Right Heart Failure from Lung Disease
▼
📌 Definition
RV enlargement/failure due to PULMONARY hypertension
(From chronic lung disease, NOT left heart disease!)
🩺 Clinical Features
  • Exercise intolerance
  • JVD
  • Hepatosplenomegaly
  • Peripheral edema
  • Tricuspid regurgitation murmur
💊 Treatment
• Reduce pulmonary pressures
• Treat underlying lung disease

Lung Cancer
The Big One
▼
🎯 Risk Factors
SMOKING = 80-90% of cases!
Radon, Asbestos, Air pollution, Genetic mutations
Type Location Key Points
NON-SMALL CELL (80-85%)
Squamous Cell Central/Hilar Strong smoking link, cavitates
Adenocarcinoma Peripheral Most common type, glandular
Large Cell Peripheral Poorly differentiated, aggressive
NEUROENDOCRINE
Small Cell (SCLC) Central AGGRESSIVE! Early metastasis, paraneoplastic
Carcinoid Central Slow-growing, better prognosis
🩺 Clinical Features
New cough, Dyspnea, Hemoptysis
Weight loss, Bone pain
Paraneoplastic syndromes (especially SCLC!)
🔬 Screening
LDCT for ages 50-80 with ≥20 pack-years
CT, PET, Bronchoscopy + biopsy for diagnosis
💊 Treatment
  • Surgery (early NSCLC)
  • Chemotherapy
  • Radiation
  • Immunotherapy
  • Targeted molecular therapy
