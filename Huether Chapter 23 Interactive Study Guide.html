🌡️ What is Hematologic Dysfunction?

Problems with blood cells that affect oxygen delivery, immune function, and clotting ability.

🔴 RBC Problems (Erythrocytes)
  • ⬇️ Too few RBCs → Anemia
  • ⬆️ Too many RBCs → Polycythemia
  • ⚠️ Abnormal structure → Hemoglobinopathies (SCA, Thalassemia)
⚪ WBC Problems (Leukocytes)
  • ⬆️ Leukocytosis → Infection, stress, inflammation
  • ⬇️ Leukopenia → Immunosuppression, bone marrow failure
  • 🎗️ Leukemia → Bone marrow infiltration
🟣 Platelet/Coagulation Problems
  • ⬇️ Too few platelets → Bleeding risk
  • ⬆️ Too many platelets → Thrombosis risk
  • ⚖️ Coagulation disorders → ↑ bleeding OR ↑ clotting
🎯 What is Anemia?
🔑
Definition: Decrease in total RBC number OR decrease in hemoglobin quality/quantity → Reduced O₂-carrying capacity → Tissue Hypoxia

Three Main Causes of Anemia:

1️⃣ Impaired RBC Production 2️⃣ Blood Loss (acute/chronic) 3️⃣ Increased RBC Destruction (hemolysis)
😰 General Symptoms of ALL Anemias
Common Symptoms
  • 😫 Fatigue, weakness
  • 💨 Dyspnea
  • 💓 Palpitations, tachycardia
  • 🌀 Dizziness, syncope
  • 👻 Pale skin, mucous membranes
  • 🤕 Headache, irritability
Severe/Acute Signs
  • ⚡ Shock
  • 🥶 Vasoconstriction
  • 💔 Organ ischemia
  • 🌡️ Low-grade fever
🔬 Morphologic Classification (SUPER HIGH-YIELD!)

💡 Memory Trick!

"-cytic" = cell SIZE | "-chromic" = hemoglobin CONTENT (color)

Macro = BIG
Micro = SMALL
Normo = NORMAL
Hypo = PALE/LOW
Type MCV MCHC Example Conditions
Macrocytic-Normochromic ⬆️ High Normal B12 deficiency, Folate deficiency, Alcoholism
Microcytic-Hypochromic ⬇️ Low ⬇️ Low Iron deficiency, Thalassemia, Sideroblastic
Normocytic-Normochromic Normal Normal Acute blood loss, Aplastic, Hemolytic, Chronic inflammation
✨
Bonus Terms: Anisocytosis = variable SIZE | Poikilocytosis = abnormal SHAPES

💪 Body's Compensatory Mechanisms

When anemia occurs, your body tries to compensate:

❤️ Cardiovascular Response +
  • ↑ Heart rate, ↑ Stroke volume
  • Hyperdynamic circulation
  • Possible murmurs, heart failure
🫁 Respiratory Response +
  • ↑ Respiratory rate, ↑ Depth
  • Dyspnea on exertion → Dyspnea at rest
🩸 Hematologic Response +
  • ↑ EPO → ↑ Reticulocytes (if bone marrow functional)
🫘 Renal Response +
  • RAAS activation → Salt + water retention
  • ↑ Plasma volume → Hemodilution
🩹 Acute Blood Loss (Posthemorrhagic Anemia)
📌
Classification: Normocytic-Normochromic (NNA)
Causes
  • 🚗 Trauma
  • 🩸 GI bleed
  • 💥 Ruptured aneurysm
Clinical Signs
  • 📉 Hypotension
  • 💓 Tachycardia
  • ⚡ Shock
  • 🥶 Cool, clammy skin
Treatment
  • 🛑 STOP the bleeding!
  • 💧 Restore volume (NS, LR, blood)
  • 🩸 Transfusion PRN
  • 💊 Iron replacement if large loss
🔬 Pathophysiology of Acute Blood Loss +
  • Loss of intravascular volume
  • Hemodilution occurs within 24 hours
  • Neutrophils & platelets increase temporarily
  • EPO increases → Reticulocytosis (if iron stores adequate)
⏰ Chronic Blood Loss
⚠️
Key Point: Eventually leads to Iron Deficiency Anemia because RBCs are lost faster than replaced!

Common Causes:

Menstrual losses GI bleeding (ulcer, malignancy)

Treatment:

Find & stop source of bleeding Iron replacement
⚙️ Megaloblastic Anemias (Macrocytic-Normochromic)
🔑
Root Cause: Impaired DNA synthesis → Large abnormal RBCs (megaloblasts)
💉 Pernicious Anemia (B12 Deficiency)
🎯 Causes of B12 Deficiency +
  • 🔬 Autoimmune destruction of Intrinsic Factor (IF)
  • ✂️ Gastrectomy
  • 💊 Long-term PPI or metformin use
  • 🦠 Ileal disease/resection
  • 🪱 Tapeworm
  • 🥗 Vegan diet (low B12)
⭐
CLASSIC SIGNS: Smooth beefy-red tongue (glossitis) + "Lemon-yellow" skin (pallor + jaundice) + NEURO SYMPTOMS

🧠 Neurological Manifestations (UNIQUE to B12!):

Paresthesias Ataxia Loss of vibration/position sense Cognitive changes
⚠️
Warning: Neuro damage may be IRREVERSIBLE!

🔬 Key Labs:

B12⬇️ Low
MMA⬆️ High (diagnostic!)
Homocysteine⬆️ High
Anti-IF antibodiesPresent
MCV⬆️ High (macrocytic)

💊 Treatment:

💉
LIFELONG B12 Replacement:
IM 1000 mcg daily/every other day × 1-2 weeks
→ Then weekly × 1-2 months
→ Then monthly FOR LIFE
Monitor for gastric cancer risk!
🥬 Folate Deficiency Anemia
🎯 Causes of Folate Deficiency +
  • 🍽️ Poor diet (elderly, alcoholism, malnutrition)
  • 🦠 Malabsorption (Crohn's, UC)
  • 🤰 Pregnancy, lactation
  • 💊 Medications: methotrexate, trimethoprim, phenytoin
⭐
KEY DIFFERENCE FROM B12: Similar symptoms BUT NO NEURO SYMPTOMS!

Clinical Signs:

Mouth ulcers Glossitis Stomatitis, cheilosis Diarrhea

Labs:

⬇️ Serum folate ⬆️ MCV Normal MMA (unlike B12!)

Treatment:

💊
Oral folate supplementation + Correct diet → Symptoms resolve in 1-2 weeks!
🎴 B12 vs Folate - Click to Flip!

How do you differentiate B12 deficiency from Folate deficiency?

3 Key Differences:

1️⃣ B12 has NEURO symptoms (folate doesn't!)

2️⃣ B12: MMA is HIGH | Folate: MMA is NORMAL

3️⃣ B12: Lifelong IM treatment | Folate: Oral, resolves quickly

🔴 Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)
🏆
MOST COMMON ANEMIA WORLDWIDE!
🎯 Causes +
  • 🩸 Chronic blood loss
  • 🍽️ Poor intake
  • 🤰 Pregnancy
  • 🦠 Malabsorption
  • 🫁 GI pathology
  • 🔥 ↑ Hepcidin (inflammation) → ↓ iron absorption

📍 Classic Clinical Signs:

Early Signs
  • 😫 Fatigue, weakness
  • 💨 Dyspnea
Late Signs
  • 👁️ Pale conjunctiva, palms
  • 🥄 Koilonychia (SPOON NAILS!)
  • 👅 Glossitis (smooth tongue)
  • 👄 Cheilosis
  • 🍽️ Dysphagia (Plummer-Vinson)
  • 🧠 Cognitive impairment (esp. children)

💡 Remember Iron Labs with "Fe Low, TIBC High!"

In IDA, the body is HUNGRY for iron → TIBC goes UP (reaching out for iron)

🔬 Diagnostic Labs:

Hgb/Hct⬇️ Low
MCV⬇️ Low (microcytic)
MCHC⬇️ Low (hypochromic)
Ferritin⬇️ LOW (KEY!)
Serum Iron⬇️ Low
TIBC⬆️ HIGH (body wants iron!)
Transferrin Saturation⬇️ Low

💊 Treatment:

💊
1️⃣ Identify and STOP blood loss
2️⃣ Oral iron therapy
3️⃣ Treat for MONTHS until ferritin is normal
🧬 Other Microcytic Anemias (Quick Notes)
🔬 Sideroblastic Anemia +

What it is: Defect in heme synthesis → Ring sideroblasts in marrow

Causes: Congenital, toxins/alcohol, medications

🧬 Thalassemias +

What it is: Genetic defect → Impaired α or β-globin chain production

(Covered more in pediatric hematology)

⏳ Anemia of Inflammation (Chronic Disease)
📌
Classification: Normocytic at first → Then microcytic if long-standing

Underlying Causes:

Infection Autoimmune disease Cancer CKD Heart Failure Chronic pulmonary disease
🔬 Pathophysiology (Important!) +

Inflammation → ↑ IL-6 → ↑ HEPCIDIN

  • Hepcidin BLOCKS iron release & absorption
  • ↓ EPO production
  • Shortened RBC lifespan

Result: Iron is "trapped" in storage → Can't be used!

💡 Key Lab Pattern to Remember!

LOW Iron + LOW TIBC + HIGH FERRITIN

Think: "Iron is trapped in storage (high ferritin), body isn't even trying to get more (low TIBC)"

🔬 Labs Comparison (IDA vs Chronic Disease):

Lab Iron Deficiency Anemia of Chronic Disease
Serum Iron⬇️ Low⬇️ Low
TIBC⬆️ HIGH⬇️ LOW
Ferritin⬇️ LOW⬆️ HIGH (KEY!)

💊 Treatment:

🎯
1️⃣ TREAT UNDERLYING CONDITION (most important!)
2️⃣ Consider EPO in CKD
3️⃣ Iron only if PROVEN deficiency
💀 Aplastic Anemia (Bone Marrow Failure)
⚠️
PANCYTOPENIA: ↓ RBCs + ↓ WBCs + ↓ Platelets (ALL THREE!)

Causes:

Acquired (Most Common)
  • 🔬 Autoimmune destruction of stem cells
  • 💊 Medications (chloramphenicol, chemo, antimetabolites)
  • ⚗️ Benzene exposure
  • ☢️ Radiation
  • 🦠 Viral infections (EBV, CMV, hepatitis)
Inherited
  • 🧬 Fanconi anemia

Clinical Manifestations (Think: What happens when ALL cells are low?)

↓ RBCs
  • 😫 Fatigue
  • 👻 Pallor
↓ WBCs
  • 🦠 Frequent infections
  • 🌡️ Fever
↓ Platelets
  • 🩸 Bleeding
  • 🔴 Petechiae

🔬 Diagnostics:

Pancytopenia on CBC HYPOCELLULAR bone marrow

💊 Treatment:

🏥
1️⃣ Remove offending toxin/drug
2️⃣ Immunosuppression (ATG, cyclosporine, steroids)
3️⃣ Stem cell transplant (younger patients)
4️⃣ Supportive transfusions
💥 Hemolytic Anemias (Increased RBC Destruction)
📌
Classification: Normocytic-Normochromic

Types of Hemolysis:

Intrinsic (RBC Defect)
  • 🔴 Membrane defects
  • ⚗️ Enzyme deficiencies (G6PD)
  • 🧬 Hemoglobinopathies (Sickle Cell)
Extrinsic (Outside RBC)
  • 🔬 Autoimmune hemolysis
  • 🦟 Infections (malaria, babesiosis)
  • ⚙️ Mechanical (prosthetic valves)
  • 💊 Drugs (oxidative stress)
  • 🩸 Transfusion reactions

📍 Classic Clinical Signs:

💡 Remember: "JAD" for Hemolysis Signs

Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes)
Anemia symptoms (fatigue, dyspnea)
Dark urine (hemoglobinuria)
Jaundice 🟡 Fatigue, dyspnea Dark urine 🟤 Splenomegaly/Hepatomegaly Tachycardia

🔬 Diagnostic Labs:

Reticulocyte count⬆️ HIGH (bone marrow trying to compensate!)
LDH⬆️ HIGH (cell breakdown)
Unconjugated bilirubin⬆️ HIGH (causes jaundice)
Haptoglobin⬇️ LOW (used up binding free Hgb)
Coombs test+ or - (immune vs non-immune)

💊 Treatment:

🎯
1️⃣ Treat the underlying cause
2️⃣ Transfusion PRN
3️⃣ Steroids for autoimmune forms
4️⃣ Avoid triggers (G6PD)
5️⃣ EPO or BMT in severe cases
