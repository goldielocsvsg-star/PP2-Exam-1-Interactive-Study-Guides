Kidney Functions - TEST FAVORITE!
What do the kidneys actually DO?
🔄 Regulatory Functions
  • Fluid balance
  • Electrolyte balance
  • Acid-base balance
  • Blood pressure regulation
🧬 Hormonal Functions
  • Erythropoietin (EPO) → RBC production
  • Renin → RAAS → BP control

Remember: Kidneys = REGULATE + HORMONE!
Without kidneys working properly:
↓ EPO = Anemia
↓ Vitamin D = Bone disease
↑ Renin = Hypertension

Glomerular Filtration
What gets filtered (and what DOESN'T!)
▼
✅ FILTERED (Small molecules)
  • Water
  • Electrolytes
  • Glucose
  • Urea
  • Creatinine
❌ NOT FILTERED (Too big!)
  • RBCs
  • Albumin
  • Proteins
125
mL/min = Normal GFR
(That's ~180 L/day filtered!)
But Only 1-3 L Excreted!
180 L filtered → Only 1-3 L comes out as urine
99% is REABSORBED!
⚙️ GFR Regulation (Autoregulation)
↓ GFR: Afferent constriction OR Efferent dilation
↑ GFR: Afferent dilation OR Efferent constriction

Autoregulation FAILS when SBP <65-70 mm Hg!
2️⃣
Tubular Reabsorption - 99% Returned!
Where most of the action happens
▼
📍 PCT = MOST Reabsorption!
Reabsorbed in PCT:
Water, Sodium, Chloride, Glucose, Amino acids, Bicarbonate
Location What Happens
Loop - Descending WATER reabsorption (permeable to water)
Loop - Ascending SODIUM reabsorption (NO water! Impermeable)
DCT & Collecting Duct ADH & Aldosterone work here!
🧬 Hormone Effects
ADH → ↑ Water permeability (reabsorb MORE water)
Aldosterone → Sodium IN, Potassium OUT
Golden Rule!
"Where SODIUM goes, WATER follows!"

Aging & the Renal System - NCLEX FAVORITE!
What changes with age?
🫘 Kidney Changes with Age
  • ↓ Nephrons
  • ↓ Cortical tissue
  • ↓ GFR
  • ↓ Urine concentrating ability
⚠️ Clinical Effects in Older Adults
• Nocturia (getting up at night to pee)
• Urgency
• DEHYDRATION risk (can't concentrate urine as well)
• DRUG TOXICITY risk (decreased clearance!)
• ↑ UTI risk
Older Adults = Watch for Drug Toxicity!
↓ GFR = Drugs cleared SLOWER = Toxicity risk!
Always consider renal function when dosing meds in elderly!

Blood Tests - MUST KNOW!
What the numbers mean
▼
Test What It Tells You Key Point
Creatinine Kidney function ↑ Creatinine = Kidney impairment!
BUN Kidney function + hydration Affected by hydration, diet, stress
Cystatin-C GFR (sensitive marker) More accurate than creatinine alone
Doubling Rule!
Doubling creatinine = 50% ↓ GFR!
This is a BIG deal!
🚨 Contrast Safety
Creatinine ≥1.5 mg/dL = AKI risk with contrast!

METFORMIN RULE:
Hold 24 hr BEFORE and 48 hr AFTER IV contrast!

Urinalysis - KEY CLUES!
What each finding means
▼
Finding Think...
Protein Glomerular damage!
Microalbuminuria EARLY kidney disease (diabetic!)
RBCs Stones, Tumors, Glomerulonephritis
WBCs + Leukocyte esterase + Nitrites UTI!
High Specific Gravity Dehydration (concentrated urine)
Low Specific Gravity CKD or Overhydration (dilute urine)

Nursing Safety Priorities
What to monitor & do
▼
🚨 Key Nursing Actions
  • Monitor urine output (<0.5 mL/kg/hr = CONCERN!)
  • Assess kidney function BEFORE contrast
  • Monitor for BLEEDING after kidney biopsy
  • Encourage hydration (unless restricted)
  • Use bladder scanner for suspected retention
