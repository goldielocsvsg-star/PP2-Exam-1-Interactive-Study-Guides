💨 Glucocorticoids

💉 Beclomethasone (Inhaled & Intranasal)
🔬 Pharm Class Inhaled corticosteroid
⚙️ MOA Decreases airway inflammation, edema, and mucus production by inhibiting inflammatory mediators
🎯 Need for Medication Long-term control of asthma and allergic rhinitis
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Oral candidiasis, hoarseness, cough, nasal irritation, headache
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Not for acute attacks. RINSE MOUTH after inhaler use. Use consistently. Use bronchodilator first if prescribed together
✅ Expected Response Decreased frequency/severity of asthma or allergy symptoms; improved lung function
💊 Prednisone
🔬 Pharm Class Systemic corticosteroid
⚙️ MOA Suppresses inflammation and immune response
🎯 Need for Medication Acute asthma exacerbations, COPD flare-ups, severe inflammation
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Hyperglycemia, weight gain, mood changes, increased infection risk, GI irritation
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Do NOT stop abruptly. Take with food. Monitor glucose, BP, signs of infection
✅ Expected Response Reduced airway inflammation, improved breathing, decreased exacerbation severity

🌬️ Beta2-Adrenergic Agonists

🚨 Albuterol (SABA - Short-Acting)
🔬 Pharm Class SABA (Short-Acting Beta Agonist) - bronchodilator
⚙️ MOA Relaxes bronchial smooth muscle via beta2 stimulation
🎯 Need for Medication RESCUE medication for acute bronchospasm
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Tremor, tachycardia, nervousness, headache, hypokalemia
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Use PRN for acute symptoms. Overuse indicates poor control. Monitor HR
✅ Expected Response RAPID relief of wheezing and shortness of breath
⏰ Salmeterol (LABA - Long-Acting)
🔬 Pharm Class LABA (Long-Acting Beta Agonist)
⚙️ MOA Long-term bronchodilation via beta2 stimulation
🎯 Need for Medication Maintenance therapy for asthma/COPD
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Tachycardia, headache, throat irritation, tremor
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations NOT for acute attacks. Use with inhaled corticosteroid
✅ Expected Response Improved symptom control and decreased nighttime symptoms

🛡️ Leukotriene Antagonists

💊 Zafirlukast
🔬 Pharm Class Leukotriene receptor antagonist
⚙️ MOA Blocks leukotrienes → decreased bronchoconstriction and inflammation
🎯 Need for Medication Prophylaxis and chronic asthma treatment
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Headache, diarrhea, liver dysfunction
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Take on EMPTY stomach. Monitor liver enzymes
✅ Expected Response Reduced asthma symptoms and fewer exacerbations
🌙 Montelukast
🔬 Pharm Class Leukotriene receptor antagonist
⚙️ MOA Prevents leukotriene-mediated inflammation
🎯 Need for Medication Asthma prophylaxis, allergic rhinitis
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Headache, GI upset, mood/behavior changes
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Take in EVENING. Monitor for neuropsychiatric changes
✅ Expected Response Improved asthma control and allergy symptom reduction

🔒 Mast Cell Stabilizer

🛡️ Cromolyn
🔬 Pharm Class Mast cell stabilizer
⚙️ MOA Prevents release of histamine and inflammatory mediators
🎯 Need for Medication Prophylaxis of asthma
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Cough, throat irritation, bronchospasm
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations NOT for acute attacks. Use consistently
✅ Expected Response Decreased frequency of asthma attacks

🚫 Anticholinergics

⚡ Ipratropium (Short-Acting)
🔬 Pharm Class Anticholinergic bronchodilator
⚙️ MOA Blocks muscarinic receptors → bronchodilation
🎯 Need for Medication COPD, adjunct in asthma
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations AVOID spraying into eyes. Use regularly
✅ Expected Response Improved airflow and reduced dyspnea
⏳ Tiotropium (Long-Acting)
🔬 Pharm Class Long-acting anticholinergic
⚙️ MOA Sustained bronchodilation
🎯 Need for Medication Maintenance treatment of COPD
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Dry mouth, constipation, tachycardia
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations NOT for rescue. Use ONCE daily
✅ Expected Response Improved exercise tolerance and fewer COPD exacerbations

☕ Methylxanthine

⚗️ Theophylline
🔬 Pharm Class Methylxanthine bronchodilator
⚙️ MOA Relaxes smooth muscle, decreases airway responsiveness
🎯 Need for Medication Chronic asthma or COPD
⚠️ Adverse Reactions N/V, insomnia, tachycardia, SEIZURES (toxicity)
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations NARROW therapeutic range. Monitor serum levels closely!
✅ Expected Response Improved airway function without toxicity

💧 Mucolytic

🌊 Acetylcysteine
🔬 Pharm Class Mucolytic
⚙️ MOA Breaks down mucus to improve clearance
🎯 Need for Medication Thick respiratory secretions
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Bronchospasm, nausea, unpleasant odor
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Have suction available. Monitor breath sounds
✅ Expected Response Thinner secretions and improved airway clearance

🩸 Oral Iron Preparations

⚙️ Ferrous Gluconate (12% elemental iron)
🔬 Pharm Class Iron supplement
⚙️ MOA Replaces iron stores; essential for hemoglobin synthesis and oxygen transport
🎯 Need for Medication Treatment and prevention of iron-deficiency anemia
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Nausea, constipation, DARK STOOLS, epigastric pain, teeth staining (liquid)
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Give 1 hr before or 2 hrs after meals. May take with food if GI upset. Avoid antacids, dairy, tea, coffee, eggs within 1 hr. Vitamin C increases absorption. Use straw for liquid. Keep away from children!
✅ Expected Response Gradual rise in hemoglobin/hematocrit; improved energy levels
💪 Ferrous Fumarate (33% elemental iron)
🔬 Pharm Class Iron supplement
⚙️ MOA Restores iron needed for RBC production
🎯 Need for Medication Iron-deficiency anemia
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Nausea, constipation, dark stools, abdominal discomfort
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Same administration guidelines as other oral iron. Monitor Hgb/Hct and reticulocyte count
✅ Expected Response Correction of anemia and replenishment of iron stores
🌟 Ferrous Sulfate (30% elemental iron)
🔬 Pharm Class Iron supplement
⚙️ MOA Provides elemental iron for hemoglobin synthesis
🎯 Need for Medication MOST commonly prescribed oral iron for iron-deficiency anemia
⚠️ Adverse Reactions GI upset, constipation, dark stools, teeth staining (liquid)
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Best absorbed on empty stomach. Educate that dark stools are expected. Monitor labs every 3-4 weeks
✅ Expected Response Increased hemoglobin within 1-2 months; full iron store replacement in 3-6 months

💉 Parenteral Iron

⚠️ Iron Dextran (IM, IV)
🔬 Pharm Class Parenteral iron supplement
⚙️ MOA Rapid replacement of iron stores when oral iron is ineffective or not tolerated
🎯 Need for Medication Severe iron-deficiency anemia or malabsorption
⚠️ Adverse Reactions ANAPHYLAXIS, hypotension, tachycardia, flushing, fever, injection-site pain, phlebitis
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations TEST DOSE REQUIRED! Monitor closely for anaphylaxis. Monitor BP and HR during IV infusion. Use Z-track method for IM injections
✅ Expected Response Improved hemoglobin and iron indices

🅱️ Vitamin B12

💉 Cyanocobalamin (IM, SubQ, Intranasal)
🔬 Pharm Class Water-soluble vitamin
⚙️ MOA Required for DNA synthesis and RBC formation
🎯 Need for Medication Pernicious anemia, B12 deficiency
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Injection site pain, hypokalemia (rare)
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Lifelong therapy may be required. Monitor potassium initially
✅ Expected Response Increased hemoglobin, improved neurologic symptoms

🍃 Folate

💊 Folic Acid / Folate
🔬 Pharm Class Water-soluble vitamin
⚙️ MOA Needed for RBC production and DNA synthesis
🎯 Need for Medication Folate-deficiency anemia, pregnancy
⚠️ Adverse Reactions Rare; rash, GI upset
👩‍⚕️ Nursing Considerations Rule out B12 deficiency BEFORE giving!
✅ Expected Response Resolution of megaloblastic anemia

🧠 Memory Tricks & Mnemonics

💨 SABA vs LABA - "SAve BAd days vs LAst BAck-up"

SABA = SAve for bad days (acute rescue)

LABA = LAsting backup (maintenance, NOT rescue)

🧼 RINSE = Remember Inhaled Needs Steroids Expectoration

Remember to Rinse mouth after inhaled corticosteroids!

Prevents oral candidiasis (thrush)

☕ Theophylline = "The-Awful-Line"

Has an AWFUL therapeutic window - very narrow!

Toxicity symptoms: TINS

  • Tachycardia
  • Insomnia
  • Nausea/vomiting
  • Seizures

🌙 Montelukast = "Mon-at-Night-lukast"

Take in the evening/night

Watch for MOOD changes!

👁️ Ipratropium = "I-protect-opium" (your eyes!)

Ipratropium = I protect my eyes!

AVOID spraying into eyes - causes blurred vision

💊 Oral Iron: "FGS" - From Gut to Stores

Ferrous Gluconate = 12% (lowest)

Ferrous Sulfate = 30% (most common)

Ferrous Fumarate = 33% (highest)

🍊 "C" is for Iron

Vitamin C = Cranks up iron absorption!

AVOID with iron: DATES

  • Dairy
  • Antacids
  • Tea
  • Eggs
  • Stuff with whole grains

💉 Iron Dextran = "Test FIRST or Anaphylaxis WORST"

Always give test dose! Risk of severe anaphylaxis

Use Z-track for IM = "Zap it deep!"

🩸 "B-FORE Folate" - B12 Before Folate!

Check B12 BEFORE giving folate

Why? Folate masks B12 deficiency → worsens neuro damage

🚫 Prednisone = "Don't STOP!"

NEVER stop abruptly! Must taper

Side effects: SWIM

  • Sugar elevated (hyperglycemia)
  • Weight gain
  • Infection risk increased
  • Mood changes

💩 Iron Stools = "Black is Back to Normal"

Dark/black stools are EXPECTED with iron!

Educate patients - this is NOT bleeding, it's NORMAL
