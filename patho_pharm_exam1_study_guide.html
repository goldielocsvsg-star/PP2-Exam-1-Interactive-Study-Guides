🫁 Asthma Medications (CRITICAL)
If they're shaking → it's working!
Albuterol causes tremors - that's EXPECTED
🚑 SABA - Albuterol (RESCUE):
  • Fast bronchodilation
  • Use PRN for acute symptoms
  • AE: tremor, tachycardia, nervousness
  • LIFESAVING!
🚨 LABA alone in asthma ↑ risk of DEATH! Always pair with ICS.
🌬️ LABA - Salmeterol:
  • NOT for acute attacks
  • MUST use with inhaled corticosteroid
  • OK alone in COPD, NEVER in asthma
💨 Inhaled Corticosteroids - Beclomethasone:
  • ↓ inflammation (prevention)
  • Takes days-weeks to work
  • Rinse mouth after use (prevent thrush)
  • Not rescue - scheduled use only
🧠 Test Tip: Use SABA 5 minutes BEFORE ICS for better delivery
💊 Systemic Corticosteroids - Prednisone:
  • For severe exacerbations
  • AE: hyperglycemia, infection risk, osteoporosis
  • NEVER stop abruptly - must taper!
🫁 Asthma Pathophysiology
Think: Tight, Inflamed, Mucus-Plugged Airways
🔬 Core Pathophysiology:
  • Chronic airway inflammation
  • Bronchoconstriction
  • Mucus hypersecretion
  • REVERSIBLE airway obstruction
🚨 Silent chest + low O₂ = COMPLETE OBSTRUCTION = EMERGENCY!
🚨 Status Asthmaticus:
  • Severe, life-threatening attack
  • Does NOT respond to usual therapy
  • Extreme dyspnea + accessory muscles
  • Sudden absence of wheezing = BAD
  • Prepare for: O₂, IV fluids, bronchodilators, steroids, intubation
💨 COPD Medications
Can't Spit, Can't Pee, Can't See
Anticholinergic side effects: dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision
🌪️ Anticholinergics - Ipratropium/Tiotropium:
  • Bronchodilation by ↓ vagal tone
  • Primarily for COPD (not asthma)
  • AE: dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision
  • DO NOT use ipratropium + tiotropium together
🚨 Theophylline: Narrow range (10-20 mcg/mL). Toxicity = seizures & dysrhythmias!
⚠️ Theophylline:
  • Narrow therapeutic index
  • Monitor levels closely
  • Smoking ↓ levels, Caffeine ↑ toxicity
💨 COPD Pathophysiology
Bronchitis = Mucus | Emphysema = Air Sacs Destroyed
🔵 Chronic Bronchitis (Blue Bloater):
  • Affects airways only
  • Productive cough
  • Frequent infections
  • Cyanosis + Edema
🌸 Emphysema (Pink Puffer):
  • Alveolar destruction
  • Loss of elastic recoil
  • Air trapping → barrel chest
  • Purse-lip breathing
🩸 Anemia Medications
Black Poop = Expected with Iron!
⚫ Iron Supplement - Ferrous Sulfate:
  • Take with vitamin C (↑ absorption)
  • AE: constipation, black stools (NORMAL)
  • Avoid antacids (↓ absorption)
🚨 Iron is TOXIC to children! Iron dextran → anaphylaxis risk (test dose!)
🔴 Vitamin B12 - Cyanocobalamin:
  • Used in pernicious anemia
  • Requires intrinsic factor
  • Deficiency causes neurologic symptoms
  • Lifelong injections if pernicious anemia
🧠 Test Tip: Folic acid can mask B12 deficiency anemia but NOT neuro damage!
🩸 Anemia Pathophysiology
📊 Morphologic Classification:
  • Microcytic hypochromic: Iron deficiency
  • Macrocytic: B12 or folate deficiency
  • Normocytic: Chronic disease, aplastic
🧬 B12 Deficiency (Pernicious Anemia):
  • Neurologic symptoms: paresthesia, gait issues
  • Requires intrinsic factor
  • Lifelong B12 injections
🫘 CKD Medications
🦴 Phosphate Binders - Sevelamer/Calcium Acetate:
  • Take WITH meals
  • Prevents phosphate absorption
  • ↓ risk of bone disease
🚨 Epoetin alfa: Risk of HTN & thrombosis! Monitor BP closely.
🩸 Anemia of CKD - Epoetin Alfa:
  • Stimulates RBC production
  • AE: hypertension, thrombosis
  • Monitor hemoglobin & hematocrit
💧 Loop Diuretic - Furosemide:
  • Watch potassium levels
  • Ototoxicity risk (hearing loss)
  • Monitor I&O, daily weights
🫘 AKI vs CKD
Before, During, After the Kidney
Prerenal, Intrarenal, Postrenal
⚡ Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):
  • Sudden ↓ kidney function
  • Prerenal: ↓ perfusion (dehydration, shock)
  • Intrarenal: kidney damage (nephrotoxins)
  • Postrenal: obstruction (stones, BPH)
🔄 Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):
  • Progressive, irreversible nephron loss
  • Fluid overload
  • Electrolyte imbalances (↑K⁺, ↑PO₄, ↓Ca²⁺)
  • Anemia (↓ erythropoietin)

🧠 Exam Strategy

Ask yourself:
1. What is the pathophysiology?
2. What drug fixes it?
3. What can go wrong? (adverse effects)
4. What do I teach?
