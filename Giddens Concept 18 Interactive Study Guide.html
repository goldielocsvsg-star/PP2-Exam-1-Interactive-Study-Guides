🫁 Concept 18: Gas Exchange

Nursing Study Guide | Patho → Assessment → Diagnostics → Interventions

🌬️ What is Gas Exchange?
🔑
Definition: Movement of O₂ INTO cells & CO₂ OUT OF cells

This requires coordination between THREE systems:

🧠 Neurologic System

Controls ventilation (breathing)

🫁 Respiratory System

Oxygenates blood

❤️ Cardiovascular System

Perfuses tissues

📚 Key Terms You MUST Know
Term Meaning
Hypoxemia Low oxygen in arterial BLOOD
Hypoxia Low oxygen reaching CELLS/TISSUES
Ischemia Insufficient oxygenated blood flow → hypoxemia → cell injury
Anoxia COMPLETE ABSENCE of oxygen in tissues

💡 Memory Trick!

Hypox-EMIA = blood (think: anemia = blood)

Hypox-IA = tissues (the "ia" stands alone like isolated tissue)

📐 Dalton's Law (Test Question!)
📝
O₂ and CO₂ diffuse INDEPENDENTLY based on their own partial pressures.

They DO NOT "swap" places or exchange each other!
👶👴 Age-Related Differences
👶 Infants
  • Surfactant sufficient after 36 weeks
  • Obligate nose breathers until ~3 months
  • Irregular breathing with short pauses (normal)
  • Smaller airways → easily obstructed → ↑ hypoxemia risk
👴 Older Adults
  • Weak respiratory muscles → weak cough
  • Less elastic alveoli → ↓ diffusion
  • ↓ RBCs → ↑ anemia risk
  • ↓ immune response → ↑ pneumonia risk
⚠️ Three Categories of Gas Exchange Problems
🌬️ 1. VENTILATION Impairment

Problems moving air IN/OUT of lungs

  • Pain → rib fracture
  • Muscle weakness → neuromuscular disease
  • Cervical spinal cord injury
  • Bronchoconstriction → asthma
  • Obstruction → chronic bronchitis, CF
  • Poor diffusion → edema, ARDS, pneumonia
🩸 2. TRANSPORT Impairment

Blood can't carry O₂ effectively (Hgb issue)

  • Anemia (low RBCs or Hgb)
  • Hemolysis (sickle cell, hemolytic anemia)
  • Acute or chronic blood loss
❤️ 3. PERFUSION Impairment

Blood FLOW is impaired

  • ↓ cardiac output
  • Thrombi / emboli
  • Vessel narrowing
  • Vasoconstriction
  • Severe blood loss
📊 Consequences of Impaired Gas Exchange
Level of Impairment Effect
Mild Fatigue (body compensates by resting)
Moderate ↑ RR, ↑ HR, hypoxemia
Severe ↓ mitochondrial activity → lactic acid → metabolic acidosis
Ventilation problem Respiratory acidosis (CO₂ retention)
Perfusion/transport problem Metabolic acidosis
Complete cessation ☠️ Anoxia → Death

👂 Auscultation - Lung Sounds

🎵 Wheezes

Sound: High-pitched, musical

Indicates: NARROWED airways (asthma, bronchospasm)

⚠️ Stridor

Sound: High-pitched, harsh (heard on INSPIRATION)

Indicates: UPPER airway obstruction - EMERGENCY!

💧 Rhonchi

Sound: Low-pitched, snoring/gurgling

Indicates: MUCUS in bronchi (may clear with cough)

✨ Crackles (Rales)

Sound: Popping, crackling (like Rice Krispies)

Indicates: FLUID in alveoli (pneumonia, HF, pulmonary edema)

💡 Memory Trick for Lung Sounds!

"Wheeze = Squeeze" (airways are squeezed/narrowed)

"Crackles = Fluid Snackles" (fluid in alveoli)

"Rhonchi = Mucus Bronchi" (mucus in large airways)

🩸 Arterial Blood Gases (ABGs) - KNOW THESE!
Value Normal Range Meaning
pH 7.35 - 7.45 ↓ = acidosis | ↑ = alkalosis
SaO₂ 95 - 100% Hemoglobin O₂ saturation
PaO₂ 80 - 100 mmHg O₂ dissolved in blood
PaCO₂ 35 - 45 mmHg ↑ = acidosis | ↓ = alkalosis (RESPIRATORY)
HCO₃ 22 - 26 mEq/L METABOLIC component
Respiratory Acidosis

CO₂ RETENTION

pH ↓ | PaCO₂ ↑

Example: COPD, hypoventilation

Respiratory Alkalosis

Blowing off CO₂

pH ↑ | PaCO₂ ↓

Example: Hyperventilation, panic attack

OBSTRUCTIVE Disease

Examples: COPD, Asthma

  • Air trapping ↑
  • ↓ FEV₁ (difficulty EXHALING)
RESTRICTIVE Disease

Examples: Pulmonary fibrosis, chest trauma, SCI

  • ↓ Tidal volume
  • Lungs can't fully EXPAND

💡 Memory Trick!

"O"bstructive = "O"ut problem (can't get air OUT)

"R"estrictive = "R"estricted expansion (can't EXPAND fully)

🛡️ Primary Prevention
🧼 Hand hygiene 🤧 Cough/sneeze etiquette 👥 Avoid crowds 🚭 SMOKING CESSATION 💉 Immunizations

Post-Op Interventions:

🌬️ Incentive spirometer 🚶 Early ambulation 🦵 DVT prevention (SCDs, anticoagulants)
💊 Medications
👃 Upper Airway Medications +
  • Antihistamines - block histamine (allergies)
  • Intranasal steroids - reduce inflammation
  • Decongestants (α1 agonists) - shrink swollen tissues
🫁 Lower Airway Medications (BRONCHODILATORS) +
  • β2 agonists (Albuterol) - RESCUE inhaler, opens airways fast
  • Anticholinergics - block bronchoconstriction
  • Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) - reduce inflammation (maintenance)
💧 Expectorants & Mucolytics +
  • Guaifenesin - thins secretions, makes cough productive
  • Acetylcysteine (Mucomyst) - breaks up thick mucus
🤫 Antitussives (Cough Suppressants) +
  • Dextromethorphan - OTC cough suppressant
  • Codeine - rarely used (opioid side effects)
⚠️
Don't suppress a PRODUCTIVE cough - need to clear secretions!
🏥 Other Interventions
🌬️ Airway Management
  • Nasopharyngeal/oropharyngeal airways
  • CPAP/BiPAP

👐 Chest Physiotherapy
  • Percussion & vibration
  • Postural drainage

🔧 Invasive Procedures
  • Chest tubes (pneumo/hemothorax)
  • Thoracentesis (remove pleural fluid)

🛏️ Positioning (HIGH-YIELD!)
Position When to Use
High Fowler's (90°) BEST for ventilation - maximizes lung expansion
Tripod COPD patients - leaning forward on arms
Good Lung DOWN Improves PERFUSION to healthy lung
Horizontal Acute lung injury
📝
NCLEX TIP: "Good lung DOWN" seems backwards but makes sense! Blood pools in the lower lung → better perfusion to the GOOD lung → better gas exchange!

🚨 COPD & Oxygen - CRITICAL!
⚠️
COPD patients: Avoid >3 L/min unless necessary!

Why? COPD patients may have a "hypoxic drive" - their stimulus to breathe is LOW O₂, not high CO₂. Too much O₂ can suppress their breathing drive!
